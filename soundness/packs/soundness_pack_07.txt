For: Childhood-onset focal seizures, progressive unilateral hemispheric atrophy, and contralateral hemiparesis.
Against: Typically presents in slightly older children (peak 6 years) with insidious, relentlessly progressive epilepsia partialis continua and gradual neurological decline, rather than an acute, permanent hemiparesis immediately following a single prolonged seizure event at age 2.

For: Acute onset of unilateral hemiparesis, seizures, and subsequent unilateral gliosis/encephalomalacia.
Against: Stroke typically produces focal/territorial encephalomalacia restricted to a specific vascular distribution (e.g., MCA territory), whereas this MRI shows generalized atrophy of the entire right cerebral hemisphere with diffuse subcortical white matter gliosis following prolonged focal seizures.

Against: No facial port-wine stain (normal systemic exam) and no leptomeningeal angiomatosis or tram-track cortical calcifications on MRI.
