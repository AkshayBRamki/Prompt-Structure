CASE: M057

KEY FINDINGS:

55-year-old male with a hard, painful 10 × 10 mm right testicular mass: Raises primary concern for a testicular neoplasm; the concurrent left epididymal mass (9 × 5 mm, hypoechoic) is an incidental spermatocele.

Scrotal ultrasound showing a well-demarcated, hyperechoic 9 × 11 mm right intratesticular mass with testicular microlithiasis: Well-demarcated intratesticular lesion; hyperechogenicity is atypical for classic seminoma and can be seen in sex cord-stromal tumors.

Immunohistochemistry positive for vimentin and calretinin, weakly positive for CD99, and negative for SALL4, OCT4, D2-40, inhibin, and AFP: Decisively excludes germ cell tumors (SALL4-, OCT4-, AFP-, D2-40-) and mesothelial lesions (D2-40-), while supporting a sex cord-stromal tumor—specifically one that frequently lacks inhibin expression.

DIFFERENTIAL:

Testicular Sertoli cell tumor (not otherwise specified)

Testicular Leydig cell tumor

Testicular germ cell tumor (e.g., seminoma)

Adenomatoid tumor of the testis

EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:

Testicular Sertoli cell tumor:

For: Occurs in middle-aged men; well-demarcated intratesticular mass with normal AFP and β-hCG; IHC profile showing positivity for vimentin, calretinin, and CD99 with negative germ cell markers (SALL4, OCT4, AFP, D2-40); unlike Leydig cell tumors, Sertoli cell tumors are inhibin-negative in roughly half of cases.

Against: Rare tumor (less than 1% of testicular neoplasms); slightly elevated LDH and testicular microlithiasis are non-specific.

Testicular Leydig cell tumor:

For: Most common sex cord-stromal tumor in adults; well-demarcated mass with normal AFP/β-hCG; typically positive for vimentin, calretinin, and CD99.

Against: Negative inhibin strongly argues against Leydig cell tumor (which is inhibin-positive in >95% of cases); absence of gynecomastia or hormonal manifestations; typically hypoechoic rather than hyperechoic on ultrasound.

Testicular germ cell tumor (e.g., seminoma):

For: Most common solid testicular tumor; associated with testicular microlithiasis and mildly elevated LDH; normal AFP and β-hCG can occur in pure seminoma.

Against: Completely excluded by negative SALL4, OCT4, D2-40, and AFP; positive calretinin and vimentin are inconsistent with germ cell origin; older age (55) and hyperechoic ultrasound appearance are less typical for seminoma.

Adenomatoid tumor of the testis:

For: Benign lesion that is strongly positive for calretinin and vimentin; normal serum tumor markers.

Against: Typically paratesticular (epididymal) rather than strictly intratesticular; negative D2-40 argues strongly against mesothelial origin (adenomatoid tumors are characteristically D2-40 positive).

EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:

Germ cell tumors are excluded by the negative SALL4, OCT4, D2-40, and AFP staining. Adenomatoid tumor (and malignant mesothelioma) is excluded by negative D2-40 and intratesticular location. Leydig cell tumor is excluded primarily by negative inhibin staining alongside the lack of endocrine signs. Committing to testicular Sertoli cell tumor, as it is the sex cord-stromal neoplasm that best accounts for the vimentin+, calretinin+, weak CD99+, inhibin-negative immunophenotype in a well-demarcated intratesticular mass.

FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Testicular Sertoli cell tumor
