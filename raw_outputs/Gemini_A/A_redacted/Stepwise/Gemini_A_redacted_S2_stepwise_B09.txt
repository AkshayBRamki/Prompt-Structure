CASE: M065
REASONING: A 27-year-old woman presents with a progressively enlarging lower abdominal mass over 3 months accompanied by fever, constitutional symptoms (anorexia, weight loss), leukocytosis, and spontaneous rupture releasing pus and blood, which strongly indicates an infectious/abscess process rather than a purely neoplastic mass. Crucially, CT imaging demonstrates an isodense lesion extending from the umbilicus to the superoanterior dome of the urinary bladder with abscess formation involving the lower abdominal wall musculature and subcutis. The anatomic tract connecting the umbilicus to the anterosuperior bladder dome is the exact course of the urachus. An infected congenital urachal remnant (urachal cyst) classically presents in young adults as a painful, enlarging midline lower abdominal mass with fever, leukocytosis, and purulent discharge, often mimicking a neoplasm or abdominal wall abscess.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Infected urachal cyst (urachal abscess)

CASE: M066
REASONING: A 30-year-old woman from a rural area with dog contact presents with acute right upper quadrant pain, high fever (39°C), guarding, and a palpable gallbladder, consistent with acute cholecystitis. Ultrasound reveals two multilocular hepatic cysts in segments IVb and VII, which—combined with her rural residence and dog exposure—are classic for hepatic hydatid cysts (Echinococcus granulosus). CT confirms a distended, thick-walled gallbladder (8 mm) with intravesicular gas adjacent to the segment IV cyst, initially raising suspicion for a cholecysto-hydatid fistula or emphysematous cholecystitis. However, intraoperative cholangiography rules out a direct cholecysto-hydatid fistula and instead identifies a cysto-biliary communication from the segment IV hydatid cyst, explaining the biliary tract involvement and secondary acute cholecystitis.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Acute cholecystitis secondary to hepatic hydatid cyst with cysto-biliary communication

CASE: M067
REASONING: A 4-month-old male dog presents with startle- and movement-induced muscle stiffness, transient motor difficulty, generalized muscle hypertrophy, and a warm-up phenomenon, with otherwise normal neurological and blood findings. Electromyography confirms classic myotonic discharges ("wax and wane" potentials). Family testing shows that the mother and 3 of 4 littermates are clinically unaffected heterozygous carriers, establishing an autosomal recessive inheritance pattern. Genetic analysis identifies a truncating mutation in exon 15 of the skeletal muscle voltage-gated chloride channel gene (CLCN1), which directly impairs chloride conductance across the sarcolemma and causes myotonia.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Autosomal recessive myotonia congenita

CASE: M068
REASONING: A term newborn develops severe, progressive unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia within the first 5 hours of life (total bilirubin rising to 310.8 μmol/L) accompanied by a significant drop in hemoglobin (142 g/L to 112 g/L), necessitating two exchange transfusions. This clinical picture indicates severe early-onset hemolytic disease of the newborn. Immune-mediated hemolysis (Coombs tests, ABO/Rh incompatibility), enzymopathies (G6PD and erythrocyte metabolism screens), and hemoglobinopathies (hemoglobin electrophoresis, Heinz body test) are all negative, narrowing the differential to a red blood cell membrane defect. Whole exome sequencing identifies a heterozygous (autosomal dominant) nonsense/missense mutation (c.82C>T in exon 2) in a red cell membrane protein gene (such as ANK1 or SPTB), which is the characteristic genetic basis of autosomal dominant hereditary spherocytosis.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Hereditary spherocytosis

CASE: M069
REASONING: A 55-year-old non-smoking male presents with a 2-month history of skin darkening and thickening (consistent with paraneoplastic acanthosis nigricans), B symptoms (evening fever, weight loss), and respiratory symptoms. Imaging reveals a large right hilar mass (71 × 39 mm) and a right-sided exudative, lymphocyte-predominant pleural effusion with elevated ADA (53 U/L) but negative microbiological testing for tuberculosis (AFB and GeneXpert negative), ruling out tuberculous pleuritis. Biopsy immunohistochemistry shows positivity for CD45, CD20, CD3, and specifically CD15, along with a high Ki-67 proliferation index (65–70%). CD15 is a hallmark marker of Reed-Sternberg cells in classical Hodgkin lymphoma (with CD45, CD20, and CD3 highlighting the mixed lymphoid background), and Hodgkin lymphoma is a well-recognized underlying cause of mediastinal/hilar masses, lymphocytic pleural effusions, and paraneoplastic acanthosis nigricans.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Hodgkin lymphoma (with paraneoplastic acanthosis nigricans)

CASE: M070
REASONING: A 28-year-old male presents with a 3-month history of swelling in the proximal phalanx of the fourth finger at the exact site of a previously treated enchondroma 7 years prior. Radiographs show a central diaphyseal lytic lesion with sclerotic margins. Histopathology demonstrates malignant features—including cellularity, spindle-shaped cells, necrosis, and mitotic figures—within a chondroid matrix. Strong S-100 positivity (80%) confirms cartilaginous differentiation, while Ki-67 positivity (10%) supports proliferative malignant activity. The development of a malignant cartilaginous tumor at the site of a preexisting enchondroma is characteristic of malignant transformation into secondary chondrosarcoma.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Secondary chondrosarcoma

CASE: M071
REASONING: A 13-year-old boy born to consanguineous parents (suggesting autosomal recessive inheritance) presents with a 3-year history of progressive ataxia, recurrent falls, spastic gait, lower-limb spasticity, brisk reflexes, and weakness without sensory or cranial nerve deficits. Brain and spine MRI show diffuse, symmetrical T2/FLAIR hyperintensities throughout the supratentorial and infratentorial white matter, prominent periventricular white matter cystic degeneration (rarefaction), and atrophy of the corpus callosum and spinal cord, without diffusion restriction or contrast enhancement. The combination of autosomal recessive juvenile-onset progressive ataxia/spasticity and diffuse leukoencephalopathy with cystic white matter degeneration is the hallmark of vanishing white matter disease (leukoencephalopathy with vanishing white matter).
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Vanishing white matter disease (leukoencephalopathy with vanishing white matter)

CASE: M072
REASONING: A 63-year-old man with known multiple myeloma presents with localized swelling, mild pain, and difficulty accessing his left chest totally implantable venous access port (TIVAP), without signs of infection. FDG PET/CT shows a new intensely hypermetabolic soft tissue mass (SUV max 15.8) encasing the port—absent on imaging 1 year earlier—alongside widespread hypermetabolic osseous lesions indicating myeloma progression. Excisional biopsy confirms a dense infiltrate of atypical kappa-restricted plasma cells, establishing a soft-tissue (extramedullary) plasmacytoma seeding or metastasizing to the venous port site.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Extramedullary plasmacytoma (metastatic plasmacytoma) at the venous access port site secondary to multiple myeloma
