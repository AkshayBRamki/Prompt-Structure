CASE: M041
REASONING: The patient is a 47-year-old male with rheumatic heart disease (status-post mitral valve replacement) who presents with a 4-week history of intermittent high-grade fever, anorexia, and weight loss, followed by acute right-sided hemiparesis. Physical examination reveals fever, a new or notable aortic diastolic murmur, a mitral prosthetic click, and Roth's spots on fundoscopy, which are classic immunologic stigmata of endocarditis. Laboratory testing shows elevated ESR and positive blood cultures from three separate sites. Echocardiography (TTE and TEE) confirms 9x9 mm vegetations on both the native aortic valve and the prosthetic mitral valve. Brain CT demonstrates multiple acute and chronic ischemic and hemorrhagic lesions (intraparenchymal hemorrhage, subarachnoid hemorrhage, and infarcts) with normal carotid angiography, consistent with septic cerebral emboli/hemorrhagic transformation secondary to valvular vegetations. Marked clinical improvement followed prolonged IV antibiotic therapy (penicillin G and gentamicin) and aortic valve replacement.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Infective endocarditis (involving the native aortic and prosthetic mitral valves) complicated by septic cerebral emboli and intracranial hemorrhage

CASE: M042
REASONING: The patient is a 43-year-old female presenting with 2 months of progressive frontal headache, unsteadiness, and mild left-sided hemiparesis. CT and MRI localize an intensely enhancing mass in the right trigone of the lateral ventricle causing peritumoral vasogenic edema, loculated enlargement of the occipital horn, and leftward midline shift. MR spectroscopy demonstrates elevated choline and decreased NAA, which supports a diagnosis of meningioma. Histopathological examination confirms fibroblastic meningioma components.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Intraventricular fibroblastic meningioma (right lateral ventricle trigone)

CASE: M043
REASONING: The patient is a 76-year-old female with chronic osteoporosis who has a 15-year history of bisphosphonate therapy followed by denosumab maintenance, as well as prior atypical insufficiency fractures of the contralateral femur and ipsilateral tibia. She now presents with 2 months of atraumatic proximal right tibial pain and localized anterior tenderness. Imaging shows cortical thickening and a horizontal fracture line in the proximal right tibia with surrounding bone marrow edema on MRI—classic radiographic hallmarks of an atypical insufficiency fracture. Laboratory studies rule out secondary metabolic bone disorders (normal PTH, calcium, phosphate, thyroid function, and vitamin D) while showing markedly suppressed osteocalcin, indicating severely suppressed bone turnover due to long-term antiresorptive therapy.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Atypical proximal tibial fracture (antiresorptive therapy-associated insufficiency fracture)

CASE: M044
REASONING: The patient is a 49-year-old female who developed postoperative acute kidney injury (creatinine 7.4 mg/dL), thrombocytopenia (platelets 114,000), and microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (hemoglobin 9.7 g/dL, elevated LDH of 714, and fragmented RBCs on peripheral smear) following surgery for hip myositis ossificans and a septic episode. This triad defines thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA). Normal ADAMTS13 activity (>50%) excludes thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP), and a negative Shiga toxin PCR excludes Shiga toxin-producing E. coli hemolytic uremic syndrome (STEC-HUS). In the setting of surgical stress and sepsis with subsequent recovery of hematologic and renal parameters, the findings point to atypical hemolytic uremic syndrome (aHUS) / secondary TMA.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Atypical hemolytic uremic syndrome (aHUS)

CASE: M045
REASONING: The patient is a 4-year-old female with 6 months of chronic hemorrhagic diarrhea, malnutrition, iron-refractory microcytic anemia, hypoalbuminemia, markedly elevated fecal calprotectin (1338 µg/g), and elevated inflammatory markers, along with a maternal history of perianal fistula. Infectious causes (negative stool cultures, negative TB and schistosomiasis workup), celiac disease, and sarcoidosis (normal ACE, urinary calcium, chitotriosidase, and chest imaging) are excluded. Ultrasound shows bowel wall thickening, mesenteric lymphadenopathy, and creeping fat. Endoscopy reveals skip-like pan-enteric involvement (focal active pangastritis, terminal ileal ulcerations, colonic aphthous lesions, and an anal fissure). Histology demonstrates chronic inflammatory changes (crypt distortion, cryptitis, crypt abscesses, Paneth cell metaplasia) and non-caseating granulomas with Langhans-type giant cells and Schaumann bodies. Given her age (under 6 years), this represents very early-onset Crohn's disease.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Very early-onset Crohn's disease

CASE: M046
REASONING: The patient is a 75-year-old female with a history of left tibial osteosarcoma presenting with rapidly progressive dysphagia over just 4 weeks and severe weight loss (7 kg). Although barium swallow ("rat-tail appearance") and high-resolution manometry (elevated LES pressure, impaired relaxation, elevated IRP) mimic primary achalasia, her advanced age, short symptom duration, rapid weight loss, and resistance at the gastroesophageal junction on EGD are classic red flags for secondary achalasia (pseudoachalasia). CT reveals a large mediastinal mass (64x84 mm) involving the esophagus, and EUS-guided biopsy confirms malignant spindle-, round-, and signet-ring-shaped tumor cells infiltrating the esophageal wall at 34 cm.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Pseudoachalasia (secondary achalasia) due to malignant mediastinal/esophageal tumor

CASE: M047
REASONING: The patient is a 19-year-old female presenting with 4 months of symmetrical proximal muscle weakness, dysphagia, and pathognomonic cutaneous findings including a photosensitive rash in a shawl distribution and bilateral Gottron's papules over the dorsal joints. Laboratory testing shows elevated inflammatory markers, elevated AST, ALT, LDH, and aldolase (despite normal CK and myoglobin), positive ANA, and a myositis antibody panel positive for both anti-SAE1 and anti-MDA5 autoantibodies. EMG, muscle MRI, and muscle biopsy (showing severe inflammatory myopathy with perifascicular pathology) definitively confirm dermatomyositis, with CT chest showing no current interstitial lung disease.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Dermatomyositis (anti-SAE1 and anti-MDA5 positive)

CASE: M048
REASONING: The patient is a 58-year-old male smoker with locally advanced, p16-negative squamous cell carcinoma of the right tongue and gum (cT4aN2cM0) with high PD-L1 expression. Echocardiography revealed a right ventricular mass initially suspected to be a thrombus, but it failed to respond to anticoagulation. Follow-up 18F-FDG PET/CT demonstrated intense hypermetabolic activity within the right ventricular mass alongside progression of the primary oral cavity tumor and nodal disease, confirming malignant cardiac involvement rather than a bland thrombus.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Cardiac metastasis (right ventricular metastasis) from oral squamous cell carcinoma
