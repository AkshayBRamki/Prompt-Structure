CASE: M017
REASONING:

A 61-year-old male presents with 3 days of progressive right iliac fossa pain, appetite loss, positive McBurney's sign, rebound tenderness, and leukocytosis, initially mimicking acute appendicitis.

CT imaging reveals a large (13 × 5.2 cm) oval mass arising from the cecum rather than simple uncomplicated appendicitis.

Postoperative pathology demonstrates a moderately differentiated (G2) malignant epithelial neoplasm infiltrating from the muscularis mucosa into the subserosa without reaching the serosa, staged as pT3N0M0.

A malignant, moderately differentiated (G2), pT3N0M0 tumor arising from the cecum is characteristic of cecal adenocarcinoma presenting as acute appendicitis.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Cecal adenocarcinoma

CASE: M018
REASONING:

A 49-year-old female presents with a 2-year history of fatigue, generalized bone pain, low bone mineral density on DXA, elevated albumin-corrected calcium (11.6 mg/dL), markedly elevated PTH (306 pg/mL), hypophosphatemia (1.6 mg/dL), and elevated urinary calcium excretion, establishing primary hyperparathyroidism.

Initial 99mTc-MIBI SPECT/CT showed uptake in the mid-left thyroid lobe, which was a false positive caused by a concurrent Hurthle cell neoplasm; low PTH washout ruled out an intrathyroidal parathyroid adenoma.

Parathyroid 4D CT localized an enhancing 8 × 6 × 12 mm lesion posterolateral to the esophagus on the left side, an ectopic retroesophageal/paraesophageal location.

Postoperative normalization of serum iPTH (17.2 pg/mL) and calcium (8.7 mg/dL) confirms that this ectopic lesion was the source of excess PTH.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Ectopic parathyroid adenoma (causing primary hyperparathyroidism)

CASE: M019
REASONING:

A 35-year-old woman presents with classic Whipple's triad: recurrent hypoglycemic symptoms (tremors, diaphoresis, palpitations, confusion) during fasting, resolution with glucose intake, and associated weight gain.

Fasting biochemical testing confirms endogenous hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia (low glucose with elevated insulin, C-peptide, and pro-insulin, suppressed β-hydroxybutyrate, and negative sulfonylurea screen).

Although conventional imaging and 68Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT were negative, 68Ga-Exendin-4 PET/CT (which targets GLP-1 receptors overexpressed on benign insulinomas) localized a 1.5-cm lesion in the pancreatic uncinate process.

Histopathology confirms a well-differentiated grade 1 pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor (Ki-67 1%) staining positive for synaptophysin and insulin, with rapid normalization of insulin levels after enucleation.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Insulinoma (pancreatic insulinoma)

CASE: M020
REASONING:

Two male patients with prior surgical resection and adjuvant imatinib therapy for high-risk gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) developed new small bowel mesenteric masses on follow-up imaging, raising suspicion for GIST recurrence.

Immunohistochemistry in both cases is negative for GIST markers (CD117, DOG-1, CD34), ruling out recurrent GIST.

Both lesions show nuclear β-catenin positivity and smooth muscle actin (SMA) positivity with a low Ki-67 index (5%), which is the hallmark immunophenotype of desmoid fibromatosis (DF), a known mimic of GIST recurrence after abdominal surgery.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Desmoid fibromatosis (mesenteric desmoid tumor)

CASE: M021
REASONING:

A 39-year-old female presents with an acute left-hemispheric ischemic stroke (right hemiparesis, aphasia, left frontal/precentral/perisylvian infarcts on MRI) preceded by a 2-month history of constitutional symptoms (intermittent fever and weight loss).

Normal MR angiography makes primary large-vessel atherosclerosis or vasculopathy unlikely and points toward a cardioembolic source.

Transthoracic and transesophageal echocardiography identify a 6 × 6 mm mitral valve vegetation on an underlying undiagnosed mitral valve prolapse with moderate mitral regurgitation, a classic predisposing structural valve abnormality.

With negative autoimmune, vasculitis, and PET-CT malignancy workups alongside prolonged fever, weight loss, a mitral valve vegetation, and septic embolic stroke, the presentation is most consistent with infective endocarditis (specifically blood culture-negative infective endocarditis).
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Infective endocarditis (blood culture-negative infective endocarditis)

CASE: M022
REASONING:

A 45-year-old male has experienced five discrete episodes of severe rhabdomyolysis (myalgias, dark urine, CK levels 22,000 to >200,000) consistently triggered by viral illnesses, resolving with IV hydration.

Extensive metabolic and neuromuscular workup ruled out fatty acid oxidation disorders, CPT1/2 deficiency, inflammatory myopathy, and structural myopathy (normal EMG and non-specific muscle biopsy).

Genetic testing identified two variants of unknown significance (VUS) in a gene associated with Emery-Dreifuss muscular dystrophy (EDMD), suggesting a possible underlying genetic susceptibility, though without clinical weakness, contractures, or myopathic biopsy/EMG changes to establish classic EDMD.

Therefore, the primary clinical diagnosis is recurrent viral-induced rhabdomyolysis (with possible underlying genetic predisposition).
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Recurrent viral-induced rhabdomyolysis

CASE: M023
REASONING:

A 13-year-old male with untreated peptic ulcer disease presents with severe, recurrent upper gastrointestinal bleeding (hematemesis, melena, anemia, hypovolemic shock) refractory to initial endoscopic management of a Forrest IIB gastric ulcer.

Follow-up CT imaging identified a vascular aneurysm arising from the gastroduodenal artery adjacent to the proximal duodenum.

Digital subtraction angiography confirmed the gastroduodenal artery aneurysm, and transcatheter embolization successfully controlled the hemorrhage.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Gastroduodenal artery aneurysm (or pseudoaneurysm) secondary to peptic ulcer disease

CASE: M024
REASONING:

A 46-year-old woman presents with a longstanding, painless, soft, mobile mass in the upper outer quadrant of the left breast.

Although mammography and MRI raised high suspicion for malignancy (BI-RADS V) due to an internal spiculated, enhancing area with microcalcifications, the overall lesion was a large (9.5 × 7.5 × 8 cm), circumscribed, predominantly fatty mass.

Core needle biopsy and final surgical excision pathology confirmed a well-circumscribed benign lesion composed of disorganized glandular (ducts and lobules), fibrous, and adipose tissues without atypia or mitotic activity, which is the defining histological appearance of a breast hamartoma (fibroadenolipoma).
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Breast hamartoma (fibroadenolipoma)
