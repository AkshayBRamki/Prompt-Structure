CASE: M113
REASONING: The patient presents with a 6-month history of progressive cough and dyspnea, restrictive ventilatory defect with impaired diffusion capacity, and hypoxemia with a widened A-a gradient. Key diagnostic findings include milky-white bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) fluid, a classic "crazy-paving" pattern on chest CT (diffuse ground-glass opacities with interlobular septal thickening), elevated LDH and tumor markers (CEA, Cyfra21-1), and lung biopsy showing granular eosinophilic, PAS-positive material filling the alveolar spaces. These features are pathognomonic for pulmonary alveolar proteinosis (PAP). The borderline-elevated anti-GM-CSF antibody level (>4 μg/mL) supports the autoimmune form of PAP. Additionally, the markedly elevated inflammatory markers (CRP, ESR), partial response to antibiotics/voriconazole, and positive bacterial culture on biopsy suggest a superimposed opportunistic pulmonary infection (such as Nocardia), a well-recognized complication of PAP.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Autoimmune pulmonary alveolar proteinosis (complicated by secondary bacterial pneumonia)

CASE: M114
REASONING: The patient presents with a 3-month history of dry cough and exertional dyspnea with right-sided cardiac pulsations and reduced breath sounds. Chest radiography demonstrates right lung hypoplasia (volume loss, ipsilateral tracheal shift, hemidiaphragmatic tenting) and cardiac dextroposition. Contrast-enhanced chest CT confirms the classic anatomical triad of Scimitar syndrome: anomalous pulmonary venous drainage from the right lung into the infradiaphragmatic inferior vena cava, right lung and right pulmonary artery hypoplasia with dextroposition of the heart, and anomalous systemic arterial supply from the abdominal aorta to the right lower lobe (along with a hyparterial bilobed right bronchus).
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Scimitar syndrome (congenital pulmonary venolobar syndrome)

CASE: M115
REASONING: This 23-year-old woman at 36 weeks gestation with twins and newly diagnosed pre-eclampsia developed acute postoperative oxygen desaturation and bilateral basal crackles following emergency cesarean section. Pulmonary embolism was excluded via CT pulmonary angiography. Chest X-ray revealed cardiomegaly and pulmonary edema, BNP was markedly elevated (6965 pg/mL) with mild troponin elevation, and echocardiography demonstrated left ventricular systolic dysfunction with an ejection fraction of 41% (<45%) and impaired diastolic function. Heart failure due to reduced LVEF (<45%) presenting near the end of pregnancy or early postpartum without another identifiable cause—particularly in the setting of major risk factors such as multiple gestation and pre-eclampsia—is diagnostic of peripartum cardiomyopathy.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Peripartum cardiomyopathy

CASE: M116
REASONING: The patient has a long-standing history of symptomatic hypoglycemia confirmed by capillary glucose (<60 mg/dL) and continuous glucose monitoring in both fasting and postprandial states, meeting Whipple's triad. Extensive endocrine and imaging workups excluded insulinoma and hyperinsulinemic states (normal insulin, proinsulin, C-peptide, and appropriate ketogenesis), adrenal insufficiency (normal cortisol), malnutrition, celiac disease, and prior gastrointestinal surgery. Given her underlying hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (hEDS) with prominent dysautonomia, POTS, and small fiber neuropathy, along with triggers including heat, fasting, meals, and exertion, her non-insulin-mediated fasting and reactive hypoglycemic episodes are best explained as dysautonomia-associated (idiopathic) hypoglycemia in the setting of hEDS.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Dysautonomia-associated idiopathic hypoglycemia (secondary to hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos syndrome)

CASE: M117
REASONING: This 30-year-old woman presents with subacute/abrupt, bilateral, painless, progressive vision loss, nyctalopia, photopsia, and peripheral visual field constriction unresponsive to antivirals and steroids. Fundoscopy shows mild disc pallor and attenuated retinal vessels without bony spicules, while FFA demonstrates inflammatory signs (pinpoint leakages, perivascular staining, and disc staining). OCT confirms photoreceptor inner/outer segment loss, and ERG shows a classic electro-negative waveform. Infectious workup and systemic malignancy screening (PET scan) are negative, excluding infectious retinopathies and paraneoplastic syndromes (CAR/MAR), and her diet makes vitamin A deficiency unlikely. Even in the absence of detectable serum autoantibodies, this clinical, imaging, and electrophysiological profile is characteristic of seronegative non-paraneoplastic autoimmune retinopathy.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Non-paraneoplastic autoimmune retinopathy

CASE: M118
REASONING: The patient presents with right-sided mouth pain, facial numbness, and dysgeusia caused by a hypermetabolic extramedullary skull base mass alongside numerous lytic bone lesions and hypermetabolic marrow involvement. Laboratory studies reveal an IgG-kappa monoclonal protein on SPEP with an elevated kappa free light chain and abnormal kappa/lambda ratio (9.2). Bone marrow biopsy demonstrates two distinct hematologic malignancies: a CD5+/CD23+ lymphoproliferative disorder explicitly identified as chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) and a clonal plasma cell neoplasm (13q14 deletion) representing multiple myeloma. Biopsy of the skull base mass confirms a plasmacytoma of distinct clonal origin from the CLL.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Coexisting multiple myeloma (with extramedullary skull base plasmacytoma) and chronic lymphocytic leukemia

CASE: M119
REASONING: This 33-week preterm neonate was born to a mother with inadequately treated gestational syphilis and has a strongly reactive nontreponemal test (RPR 1:128). Although asymptomatic on physical examination, long bone radiographs reveal classic skeletal hallmarks of early congenital syphilis: metaphyseal osteochondritis and periostitis, including radiolucent transverse bands with adjacent sclerotic lines, irregular sclerotic metaphyseal borders of the distal radius and ulna, and a transverse osteolytic lesion in the proximal femoral metaphysis.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Congenital syphilis

CASE: M120
REASONING: The patient presents with acute severe abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, and a palpable abdominal mass. CT imaging reveals absence of the spleen in its normal left upper quadrant position, an ectopic hypodense nonenhancing mass in the mid-abdomen/right iliac fossa, and twisted splenic vessels forming a classic "whirlpool sign." Surgical exploration confirms an ectopic spleen with a 720-degree torsion of its vascular pedicle, resulting in splenic infarction requiring splenectomy.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Torsion and infarction of a wandering spleen
