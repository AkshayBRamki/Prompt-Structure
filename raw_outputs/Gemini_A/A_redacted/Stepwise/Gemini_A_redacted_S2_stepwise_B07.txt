CASE: M049
REASONING:

The patient is a 17-year-old male with progressive right knee pain, swelling, functional impairment, and ipsilateral muscular atrophy without significant trauma.

He has a relevant past medical history of a biopsy-confirmed synovial haemangioma of the right ankle 11 years earlier.

Radiographs show diminished bone mineralization, epiphyseal overgrowth, intercondylar groove widening, subchondral sclerosis, and joint space narrowing—classic features of hemophilia-like arthropathy caused by recurrent intra-articular bleeding (hemarthrosis).

MRI reveals lobulated, coalescent vascular lesions infiltrating the suprapatellar and Hoffa's fat pads with marked gadolinium enhancement and arterial supply from popliteal branches, along with secondary degenerative changes.

Excisional biopsy confirms spindle cell haemangioma, establishing a synovial haemangioma of the spindle cell subtype involving the right knee.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Synovial spindle cell haemangioma of the right knee

CASE: M050
REASONING:

The patient is an 86-year-old female with a history of rectal carcinoma, chronic kidney disease, valvular heart disease, and asthma presenting with sudden-onset sleepiness and subsequent acute respiratory deterioration with diffuse wheezing.

Objective findings include hypoxemia (SpO2 93% on room air, PaO2 58 mmHg), atrial fibrillation, bilateral pleural effusions, and severe pulmonary hypertension (pulmonary artery systolic pressure of 81 mmHg) with reduced ejection fraction on echocardiogram.

Brain CT is normal, ruling out an acute intracranial cause for her sudden-onset sleepiness, which can be an atypical presentation of hypoxemia or hemodynamic compromise in elderly patients.

Given her malignancy history (hypercoagulable state), sudden respiratory deterioration, hypoxemia, severe pulmonary hypertension, and definitive identification on a contrast-enhanced chest CT, the primary acute diagnosis is pulmonary embolism.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Pulmonary embolism

CASE: M051
REASONING:

The patient is an 88-year-old male presenting with severe back pain, epistaxis, significant weight loss, elevated liver enzymes, markedly elevated lipase (1,592 U/L), and progressive cholestasis (rising bilirubin and alkaline phosphatase).

Imaging demonstrates a pancreatic mass causing biliary obstruction with widespread metastases (liver, bones, spleen, adrenal glands, peritoneum, retroperitoneum, and mediastinal/hilar lymph nodes).

Normal CA19-9 levels argue against typical pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma.

Biopsy of the retroperitoneal mass shows strong positivity for epithelial markers (AE1/AE3, CAM5.2, CK7), neuroendocrine markers (synaptophysin, chromogranin A), and TTF-1, with an extremely high proliferation index (Ki-67 of 95%), which is diagnostic of a high-grade (poorly differentiated) small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma, originating from the pancreas.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Metastatic pancreatic small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinoma)

CASE: M052
REASONING:

The patient is a 16-year-old girl presenting with a one-year history of intermittent upper abdominal pain, an upper abdominal mass, painless jaundice, nausea, vomiting, and dark ("tea-colored") urine.

Labs demonstrate conjugated hyperbilirubinemia and elevated transaminases, consistent with obstructive jaundice.

Ultrasound, MSCT, and MRCP demonstrate bilateral intrahepatic and extrahepatic fusiform cystic biliary dilations connected to the extrahepatic bile duct.

According to the Todani classification, multiple cystic dilations involving both the intrahepatic and extrahepatic biliary tree define a Type IVA choledochal cyst.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Choledochal cyst (Todani type IVA)

CASE: M053
REASONING:

The patient is a 47-year-old female presenting with decreased responsiveness, decreased appetite, hyponatremia, and hypoglycemia, alongside a lifelong history of delayed milestones, primary amenorrhea, progressive hearing loss, and visual impairment.

Physical examination shows classic stigmata of Turner syndrome (short stature at 144 cm, short neck, low posterior hairline, broad chest, absent axillary/pubic hair, sensorineural hearing loss) along with bilateral optic atrophy and immature cataracts.

Pelvic ultrasound reveals a hypoplastic uterus with non-visualized ovaries.

Karyotype analysis shows 46,X,i(X)(q10)(37)/45,X(3), confirming mosaic Turner syndrome with an isochromosome of the long arm of the X chromosome.

Additionally, hormonal assays show hypopituitarism (low FSH, LH, GH, ACTH, and cortisol), explained by the brain MRI findings of a suprasellar meningioma extending into the pituitary gland (as well as a second meningioma in the left cerebellopontine angle).
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Mosaic Turner syndrome (complicated by hypopituitarism secondary to suprasellar and cerebellopontine angle meningiomas)

CASE: M054
REASONING:

The patient is a 50-year-old female presenting with severe right ankle pain and inability to bear weight after falling backward on stairs.

Physical exam reveals tenderness and swelling over the posterior aspect of the medial malleolus with pain on passive dorsiflexion, an intact neurovascular exam, and no proximal fibular tenderness (excluding a Maisonneuve fracture).

X-rays and CT scan confirm an obliquely oriented, minimally displaced fracture of the posterior malleolus involving 40% of the tibiotalar articular surface, with no other associated tibial or fibular fractures.

The fracture required open reduction and internal fixation due to the large articular surface involvement (>25%).
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Isolated posterior malleolus fracture of the right ankle

CASE: M055
REASONING:

The patient is a 45-year-old male presenting with painless jaundice, dark urine, and cholestatic liver enzyme elevation (alkaline phosphatase 546 U/L, bilirubin 8.7 mg/dL).

CT and MRI/MRCP reveal a long pancreaticobiliary common channel (~15 mm), indicating an anomalous pancreaticobiliary junction (pancreaticobiliary maljunction), along with a 50 mm fusiform dilation of the common bile duct (choledochal cyst) and a mass in the common hepatic duct.

Both anomalous pancreaticobiliary junction and choledochal cysts strongly predispose patients to biliary tract malignancy.

ERCP biopsy and final surgical pathology confirm a poorly differentiated adenosquamous carcinoma of the common hepatic duct (cholangiocarcinoma) arising from an intraductal tubular papillary neoplasm.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Adenosquamous carcinoma of the common hepatic duct (cholangiocarcinoma) arising from an intraductal tubular papillary neoplasm, associated with a choledochal cyst and anomalous pancreaticobiliary junction

CASE: M056
REASONING:

The patient is a 34-year-old pregnant woman (IVF conception) whose fetus was diagnosed with suspected congenital small bowel (jejunal) atresia on prenatal ultrasounds showing stomach and jejunal dilatation.

Fetal MRI at 33 weeks 3 days confirmed the intestinal atresia and revealed a teardrop-shaped hematoma adjacent to the umbilical cord, indicative of an umbilical cord ulcer.

She subsequently presented with decreased fetal movement, and emergency ultrasound confirmed intrauterine fetal demise (no fetal heart rate).

Post-delivery and postmortem findings of large-volume bloody amniotic fluid (1300 mL), a pale (exsanguinated) fetus, and gross umbilical cord ulcerations with decreased Wharton's jelly and hemorrhagic vessels confirm fetal hemorrhage from umbilical cord ulceration secondary to congenital jejunal/small bowel atresia.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Umbilical cord ulceration associated with congenital small bowel (jejunal) atresia causing intrauterine fetal demise
