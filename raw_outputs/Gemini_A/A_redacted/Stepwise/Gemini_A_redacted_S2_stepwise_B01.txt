CASE: M001
REASONING: The patient is a 37-year-old woman with a history of a resected left perineal schwannoma (with local recurrence) who now presents with multiple homogeneously enhancing, bead-like intradural/intraspinal lesions in the lumbosacral vertebral canal (L1–S1), histologically confirmed as schwannomas. The presence of multiple pathologically proven schwannomas involving both spinal nerve roots and peripheral nerves without vestibular schwannomas (normal head imaging) and without an NF2 mutation rules out neurofibromatosis type 2 (NF2-related schwannomatosis) and meets the clinical diagnostic criteria for schwannomatosis. Because genetic testing is negative for NF2, SMARCB1, and LZTR1 mutations and the family history is negative, this represents sporadic (or non-NF2/NOS) schwannomatosis.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Schwannomatosis (sporadic schwannomatosis)

CASE: M002
REASONING: The patient is a 64-year-old woman diagnosed with bilateral, poorly differentiated, triple-negative (ER-, PR-, HER2-) invasive ductal carcinomas of the breast (left pT1c/G3/N0, right pT1b/G3/N0) with a high Ki-67 index (50%). Postoperative CT revealed a spiculated pulmonary nodule, adrenal metastasis, and subcutaneous nodules. Biopsy of the lung lesion showed carcinoma with histological features similar to her breast neoplasms. Immunohistochemistry of the lung biopsy was CK7-positive and negative for all primary lung lineage markers (TTF-1 and Napsin A for lung adenocarcinoma; p40 for squamous cell carcinoma), and molecular testing was wild-type for all major non-small cell lung cancer oncogenic drivers (ALK, EGFR, ROS1, RET, METex14, NTRK, BRAF). Although GATA3 was negative, loss of GATA3 expression is common in high-grade triple-negative breast carcinomas. Together, the matching histomorphology, CK7-positive/TTF-1-negative/Napsin A-negative/p40-negative immunoprofile, and absence of lung driver mutations confirm pulmonary metastasis from her triple-negative breast cancer.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Metastatic triple-negative breast cancer

CASE: M003
REASONING: The patient is a 2-year-old boy with a 1.5-year history of progressive, painless abdominal distension and a large, non-tender, partially reducible, positively transilluminating left inguinoscrotal swelling. Imaging demonstrates a massive (25 × 30 cm) multiloculated, hypodense cystic lesion with internal septations extending from the abdomen through the left inguinal canal into the scrotum while displacing bowel loops posteriorly. While an abdominoscrotal hydrocele is in the differential diagnosis for a transilluminating abdominoscrotal mass in a child, a huge multiloculated, septated cystic mass displacing retroperitoneal/abdominal structures that requires histopathological confirmation is characteristic of a cystic lymphangioma extending through the inguinal canal.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Abdominoscrotal cystic lymphangioma (cystic lymphangioma)

CASE: M004
REASONING: The patient is a 51-year-old premenopausal woman with a well-circumscribed, 2.4-cm left breast mass, elevated serum CEA (60.1 ng/mL), and no distant or nodal metastases on PET/CT and sentinel lymph node biopsy. Histopathology demonstrates a high-grade infiltrating adenocarcinoma with prominent mucinous features (mucus secretion) and papillary architecture. Unlike conventional mucinous (colloid) breast carcinoma—which is typically low-grade and hormone receptor-positive—this tumor is triple-negative (ER-, PR-, HER2-) with a high Ki-67 index (70%) and strong p53 expression. Its immunohistochemical profile (CK7-positive; CK20-, CDX-2-, SATB2-, and PAX-8-negative; weakly positive GCDFP-15 and mammaglobin) rules out metastatic gastrointestinal, pancreaticobiliary, and ovarian mucinous carcinomas and confirms a primary breast origin. This clinical, morphological, and immunohistochemical triad is classic for primary mucinous cystadenocarcinoma of the breast.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Primary mucinous cystadenocarcinoma of the breast

CASE: M005
REASONING: The patient is an immunocompetent 68-year-old woman (normal CBC, blood glucose, HbA1c, and negative HIV serology) presenting with acute odynophagia, severe retrosternal burning pain, and hematemesis. Upper endoscopy shows multiple well-circumscribed, "volcano-like" ulcers with erythematous borders and central depressions in the mid-esophagus surrounded by normal mucosa, which is the classic endoscopic appearance of herpes simplex virus (HSV) esophagitis. Biopsy histopathology confirms viral cytopathic effects characteristic of HSV (multinucleation, nuclear enlargement, and ground-glass nuclei). The initially negative IgM/IgG serology followed by IgG seroconversion at 2 months confirms primary HSV infection.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Primary herpes simplex virus (HSV) esophagitis

CASE: M006
REASONING: The patient is a 32-year-old woman with persistent periumbilical pain, nausea, and vomiting found to have a 5 × 6 cm oval-shaped gastric mass along the lesser curvature initially suspected to be a gastrointestinal stromal tumor (GIST) on CT. Histopathology from endoscopic ultrasound-guided biopsy reveals a dense spindle cell proliferation. Immunohistochemistry is strongly positive for S100 and negative for GIST markers (CD117, DOG-1, CD34) as well as smooth muscle markers (SMA, Desmin). This immunohistochemical profile definitively excludes GIST and leiomyoma/leiomyosarcoma and establishes the diagnosis of a gastric schwannoma.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Gastric schwannoma

CASE: M007
REASONING: The patient is an asymptomatic 30-year-old primigravida presenting for routine antenatal care at 12 weeks of gestation. Ultrasound shows an empty endometrial cavity with a gestation located near the left uterine horn, and MRI confirms a live 12-week fetus (crown-rump length 5.8 cm) located within the left fallopian tube. Because the patient has no abdominal pain, tenderness, guarding, or hemodynamic instability, this represents an unruptured advanced first-trimester tubal (likely interstitial/cornual) ectopic pregnancy.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Unruptured ectopic pregnancy (left tubal/interstitial ectopic pregnancy)

CASE: M008
REASONING: The patient is a 63-year-old woman with metastatic small bowel neuroendocrine tumor (SBNET) involving the mesentery who had several months of postprandial abdominal pain and decreased oral intake (classic for chronic mesenteric ischemia/intestinal angina) and now presents with abrupt-onset, severe (10/10) abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, and leukocytosis. CT imaging demonstrates decreased bowel wall enhancement and wall thickening in the right lower quadrant small bowel loops in the setting of tumor encasement/involvement of the superior mesenteric artery (SMA) and superior mesenteric vein (SMV). The acute deterioration on top of chronic postprandial pain with CT evidence of bowel hypoperfusion confirms acute-on-chronic mesenteric ischemia secondary to mesenteric SBNET involvement.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Acute-on-chronic mesenteric ischemia (acute mesenteric ischemia secondary to small bowel neuroendocrine tumor)
