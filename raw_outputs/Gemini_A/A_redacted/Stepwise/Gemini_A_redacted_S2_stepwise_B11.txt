CASE: M081
REASONING: The patient is a 16-year-old male presenting with a progressive neuropsychiatric and movement disorder (agitation, slurred speech, spasticity, chorea, rigidity, generalized tonic-clonic seizures, and cognitive decline with a MoCA of 22/30). Imaging via spiral CT demonstrates bilateral calcifications in the basal ganglia, thalami, and subcortical white matter. Crucially, copper and calcium metabolism studies ruled out key secondary causes of intracranial calcification and movement disorders such as Wilson's disease and pseudohypoparathyroidism, and there is no family history of neurological disease. The triad of progressive neuropsychiatric/extrapyramidal symptoms, bilateral symmetrical basal ganglia and subcortical calcifications, and exclusion of metabolic/secondary causes is diagnostic of Fahr's disease (idiopathic basal ganglia calcification).
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Fahr's disease (Idiopathic basal ganglia calcification)

CASE: M082
REASONING: This 58-year-old male presents with a multisystem necrotizing and destructive process involving the upper respiratory tract (chronic sinusitis, epistaxis, midline septal defect, destruction of the orbital floor and hard palate, proptosis), lower respiratory tract (cavitary lung lesions unresponsive to antibiotics), and skin (painful lower extremity ulcers with raised violaceous borders confirmed as pyoderma gangrenosum on biopsy, flaring upon prednisone taper). Although intranasal cocaine use can cause cocaine-induced midline destructive lesions (CIMDL), CIMDL does not typically account for cavitary pulmonary lesions and systemic steroid-responsive pyoderma gangrenosum. While ANCA serology is negative and biopsy findings are nonspecific, ANCA-negative granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA) is well recognized and classically presents with this exact combination of sinonasal/orbital destruction, cavitary lung lesions, and pyoderma gangrenosum-like cutaneous ulcerations.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: ANCA-negative granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA)

CASE: M083
REASONING: A previously healthy, fully immunized toddler presents with an acute upper respiratory prodrome (low-grade fever, cough, cold) rapidly progressing to lower respiratory tract distress characterized by tachypnea (RR 62/min), subcostal retractions, and bilateral wheezing. Chest X-ray confirms bilateral hyperinflated lung fields, which is the hallmark radiographic finding of acute viral bronchiolitis. With sterile blood cultures and a negative RSV test, the nasopharyngeal RT-PCR returning positive with a cycle threshold (Ct) value of 28 strongly points to SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19) as the causative pathogen of acute viral bronchiolitis.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Acute bronchiolitis due to SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19) infection

CASE: M084
REASONING: The 38-year-old male has a 20-year history of generalized tonic-clonic seizures with an otherwise unremarkable physical and neurological examination. Brain MRI demonstrates a specific congenital midline anomaly: absence of the posterior portion of the septum pellucidum, mild prominence of the lateral ventricles, and inferior displacement of the crura of the fornix without commissural formation. Because there are no other associated structural anomalies (such as optic nerve hypoplasia or pituitary defects that would indicate septo-optic dysplasia), the findings are diagnostic of isolated partial agenesis of the septum pellucidum.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Isolated partial agenesis of the septum pellucidum

CASE: M085
REASONING: The 66-year-old male with chronic hepatitis B presents with abdominal distension and a large, heterogeneous, necrotic/hemorrhagic hepatic mass with normal epithelial/hepatocellular tumor markers (AFP, CEA, CA 19-9). Post-surgical pathology reveals a biphasic mesenchymal malignancy consisting of two distinct components: well-differentiated spindle cells expressing smooth muscle markers (desmin, caldesmon, and SMA), confirming leiomyosarcoma, juxtaposed with a high-grade dedifferentiated atypical cell component showing high mitotic activity, necrosis, and elevated Ki-67. This biphasic histology is diagnostic of primary hepatic dedifferentiated leiomyosarcoma.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Primary hepatic dedifferentiated leiomyosarcoma

CASE: M086
REASONING: A 66-year-old female presents with a 4-month history of lower abdominal pain, anorexia, weight loss, elevated CA19-9/LDH/CRP, and a large (15 × 12 × 7 cm) necrotic, ruptured right ovarian mass (FIGO stage IIIC). Histopathology shows a high-grade spindle cell sarcoma alongside features of a teratoma with malignant transformation. Immunohistochemistry demonstrates positivity for smooth muscle actin (SMA) and negativity for S100, confirming smooth muscle differentiation and ruling out neural/melanocytic sarcomas. Therefore, the diagnosis is ovarian leiomyosarcoma arising from malignant transformation of a teratoma.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Ovarian leiomyosarcoma arising from a mature cystic teratoma

CASE: M087
REASONING: A 58-year-old woman with a history of treated H. pylori gastritis and a previously noted hyperplastic polyp presents with a 26-mm lobulated, elevated gastric lesion near the esophagogastric junction that mimics a submucosal tumor on endoscopy and EUS. While FNA initially suggested an inflammatory fibroid polyp, postoperative histopathology rules that out (no eosinophilic infiltration) and demonstrates downward (inverted) proliferation of hyperplastic gastric crypt epithelium into the submucosal layer. This classic pattern of inverted mucosal growth into the submucosa mimicking a submucosal tumor is diagnostic of a gastric inverted hyperplastic polyp.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Gastric inverted hyperplastic polyp

CASE: M088
REASONING: A 5-year-old boy presents with recurrent focal seizures of occipital origin, evidenced by visual aura (blurred vision), bilateral horizontal nystagmus, brief clonic extremity movements, and preserved consciousness, corroborated by right occipital-to-posterior-temporal/parietal epileptiform activity on EEG. Brain MRI reveals a cortical, gyral-expanding ("bulging gyrus") lesion in the right parieto-occipital lobe that is isointense on T1 and slightly hyperintense on T2/FLAIR. In a young child with focal occipital epilepsy and an otherwise normal neurological exam, a cortical-based, gyral-expanding lesion with these MRI characteristics is most consistent with a dysembryoplastic neuroepithelial tumor (DNET).
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Dysembryoplastic neuroepithelial tumor (DNET)
