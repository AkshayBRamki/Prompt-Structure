CASE: M097
REASONING: The patient is an 83-year-old female presenting with progressive dementia initially diagnosed as Lewy Body Dementia, complicated by late-onset somatic dyskinesia (diaphragm and abdomen), akathisia, Parkinsonism, complex mood disorder, sleep disturbance, and psychosis with confabulations. Head CT demonstrates marked cerebral atrophy with slight pallidal calcifications. Genetic testing shows a normal number of CAG repeats in the HTT gene, excluding Huntington's disease. Crucially, 16S metagenomic sequencing reveals the presence of the Spirochaetes phylum, overwhelmingly composed of Treponema species (90%), specifically oral spirochetes such as Treponema medium (42%), Treponema sp. (31%), and Treponema denticola (20%). The detection of these non-syphilitic treponemes in the setting of severe, atypical neuropsychiatric degeneration and dementia points to a spirochetal CNS infection/involvement.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Neurospirochetosis (Treponema-associated dementia)

CASE: M098
REASONING: The patient is a 23-year-old woman with a 10-year history of a severe, Hurley stage III chronic inflammatory skin condition treated with clindamycin, rifampin, doxycycline, and adalimumab. Physical examination confirms actively draining nodules and sinus tracts in classic intertriginous areas (bilateral axillae, inguinal folds, and mons pubis), which are pathognomonic for hidradenitis suppurativa. Although she also presents with systemic inflammatory features (fevers, weight loss, leukocytosis, hypercalcemia, elevated IgG/IgG4, and sialadenitis), biopsy of the cervical lymph nodes and parotid gland shows fewer than 5% IgG4/IgG-positive cells, ruling out IgG4-related disease. PET/CT confirms hypermetabolic cutaneous thickening consistent with severe flares of the underlying dermatologic condition.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Hidradenitis suppurativa (Hurley stage III)

CASE: M099
REASONING: The patient is a 64-year-old male with a history of long-term statin therapy (atorvastatin followed by rosuvastatin) who developed a 3-month history of progressive proximal muscle weakness and severe myalgia. Laboratory testing reveals markedly elevated creatine kinase and transaminases, while EMG shows diffuse myopathy consistent with myonecrosis. Muscle biopsy demonstrates muscle fiber necrosis and regeneration with minimal inflammatory infiltrate, distinguishing it from polymyositis or dermatomyositis. Finally, strongly positive anti-HMGCR antibodies (>200 CU) with negative anti-SRP antibodies confirm the diagnosis of statin-associated autoimmune necrotizing myopathy.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Statin-induced immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy (anti-HMGCR myopathy)

CASE: M100
REASONING: The patient is a 3-year-old girl with normal early development who experienced prolonged focal seizures affecting the left side at age 2, immediately followed by persistent contralateral spastic left hemiparesis and subsequent psychomotor delay. Brain MRI reveals generalized atrophy of the right cerebral hemisphere with ipsilateral (homolateral) ventricular dilatation and subcortical white matter gliosis. This classic triad of prolonged unilateral seizures in early childhood, permanent contralateral hemiparesis, and unilateral cerebral hemiatrophy is characteristic of hemiconvulsion-hemiplegia-epilepsy (HHE) syndrome.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Hemiconvulsion-hemiplegia-epilepsy (HHE) syndrome

CASE: M101
REASONING: The patient is a 13-year-old female presenting with a 2-week history of signs and symptoms of raised intracranial pressure (headache worse on straining, nausea, vomiting, hypertension with relative bradycardia) and cerebellar dysfunction (ataxia, dizziness, vertigo). Brain MRI reveals a large, heterogeneously enhancing midline posterior fossa mass arising from the cerebellar vermis and roof of the fourth ventricle, causing obstructive hydrocephalus and brainstem compression. The lesion demonstrates restricted diffusion (bright on DWI, low on ADC)—a hallmark of high cellularity typical of embryonal tumors—along with SWI blooming (hemorrhage or calcification). In a pediatric patient, a midline vermian/fourth ventricular roof mass with restricted diffusion is classic for medulloblastoma (accompanied here by incidental craniovertebral junction anomalies including basilar invagination and occipitalization of C1).
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Medulloblastoma

CASE: M102
REASONING: The patient is a previously healthy 8-year-old boy who presented with a 3-day viral prodrome (high fever, fatigue, vomiting, abdominal pain) that rapidly deteriorated into acute decompensated heart failure and cardiogenic shock (hypotension, oliguria, confusion, muffled heart sounds, gallop rhythm, bilateral rales, and pulmonary edema on chest X-ray). Cardiac biomarkers (troponin I, CK-MB, BNP, NT-proBNP) are elevated, ECG shows severe conduction abnormalities and ventricular irritability (wide QRS, complete RBBB, ST depression, PVCs), and echocardiography reveals severely reduced ejection fraction (28%) without ventricular dilation or hypertrophy, indicating an acute myocardial injury. Negative blood cultures rule out bacterial sepsis, while PCR and mNGS identify human rhinovirus A66 (HRV-A66) as the causative pathogen.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Fulminant viral myocarditis (caused by human rhinovirus A66)

CASE: M103
REASONING: The patient is a 27-year-old woman with a history of recent abdominal trauma (with lumbar fracture) presenting with chronic abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, and a palpable abdominal mass. Imaging shows a large, well-defined peritoneal cystic mass with T1 and T2 hyperintensity and a dark peripheral T2 rim, consistent with hemorrhagic content and a fibrous/hemosiderin-laden wall. Normal liver, spleen, pancreas, and ovaries exclude pancreatic pseudocyst and ovarian origin, and normal tumor markers argue against malignancy. Histopathology confirms a thin fibrous wall containing chronic inflammation, necrotic debris, and blood clots with no epithelial lining and no neoplastic cells, which is diagnostic of a post-traumatic pseudocyst of the peritoneum/mesentery.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Post-traumatic peritoneal pseudocyst

CASE: M104
REASONING: The patient is a 7-year-old boy presenting with status epilepticus following a 6-month history of progressive neurological deterioration, including visual loss, hearing impairment, cognitive decline, pseudobulbar affect, and spastic quadriparesis. Physical examination also reveals diffuse mucocutaneous hyperpigmentation (gums, perioral area, knuckles, joints), a classic sign of primary adrenal insufficiency, which is confirmed by markedly elevated serum ACTH (5 times normal). Brain MRI shows severe bilateral parieto-occipital white matter demyelination (Loes score 22/34). Elevated very long chain fatty acids (C24, C26, and elevated C24/C22 and C26/C22 ratios) definitively confirm X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy, while normal lactate and negative glycosphingolipid markers exclude mitochondrial disorders and Krabbe disease.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Childhood cerebral X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy (X-ALD) with primary adrenal insufficiency
