CASE: M057
REASONING:

A 55-year-old male presents with a hard, painful, well-demarcated hyperechoic right testicular mass (9 × 11 mm) with testicular microlithiasis and normal serum AFP and β-hCG levels (with only mildly elevated LDH).

Negative immunohistochemistry for SALL4, OCT4, AFP, and D2-40 effectively rules out germ cell tumors (such as seminoma, embryonal carcinoma, and yolk sac tumor) as well as mesothelial/adenomatoid tumors (typically D2-40 positive).

Positivity for vimentin, calretinin, and weak CD99 supports a sex cord-stromal tumor of the testis.

Among testicular sex cord-stromal tumors, Leydig cell tumors are almost universally positive for inhibin, whereas Sertoli cell tumors are frequently inhibin-negative while staining positive for vimentin, calretinin, and CD99.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Testicular Sertoli cell tumor

CASE: M058
REASONING:

An 89-year-old male with Waldenstrom macroglobulinemia presents with progressive dyspnea, orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, peripheral edema, and notable dysphagia to both solids and liquids with coughing and emesis upon oral intake.

Imaging (chest X-ray, CT, and echocardiogram) demonstrates cardiomegaly and a large pericardial effusion without classic initial signs of cardiac tamponade (no JVD, pulsus paradoxus, electrical alternans, or low-voltage QRS), and pericardial fluid cytology is negative for malignancy.

A large pericardial effusion can exert external mass effect on the adjacent esophagus (and left atrium), resulting in mechanical esophageal compression and dysphagia, which explains the combination of heart failure/effusion symptoms and dysphagia that prompted pericardiocentesis.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Large pericardial effusion (causing esophageal compression and dysphagia)

CASE: M059
REASONING:

A 24-year-old male presents with a slow-growing, painless right medial upper eyelid mass over 10 years causing inferotemporal globe displacement without visual acuity loss or motility restriction.

MRI shows a well-circumscribed, homogeneously enhancing medial orbital mass extending from the extraconal into the intraconal space.

Histopathology demonstrates a benign monomorphic spindle-cell neoplasm with characteristic cigar-shaped nuclei, dispersed calcifications, and no significant mitotic activity or necrosis.

Immunohistochemistry is positive for smooth muscle markers (SMA and desmin) and negative for S100 (excluding neural tumors like schwannoma) and CD34 (excluding solitary fibrous tumor), confirming a benign smooth muscle tumor of the orbit.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Orbital leiomyoma

CASE: M060
REASONING:

A 57-year-old woman on warfarin presents with fever, headache, and mild left-sided hearing loss.

Brain MRI and non-contrast CT reveal a ring-enhancing, cystic, hemorrhagic left hemispheric lesion with an air-fluid level, which is characteristic of a gas-forming brain abscess (with hemorrhage likely exacerbated by therapeutic anticoagulation, INR 2.89).

CSF analysis shows pleocytosis, elevated protein, and hypoglycorrhachia, supporting an intracranial bacterial infection.

Anaerobic culture points to Clostridium species—classic gas-forming anaerobes capable of causing gas-containing brain abscesses—which was definitively confirmed at the species level by molecular sequencing (most commonly Clostridium perfringens).
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Clostridium brain abscess (gas-forming hemorrhagic brain abscess)

CASE: M061
REASONING:

A 2-year-old male presents with acute-onset bilateral upper extremity pseudoparalysis (flaccid arms with preserved finger movement and intact sensation) following an unwitnessed event at daycare.

Extensive imaging (plain radiographs of the cervical spine and upper extremities, head/cervical spine CT, and chest X-ray) rules out fractures, dislocations, and central nervous system or spinal cord injury.

Complete resolution of symptoms immediately after bilateral elbow supination and flexion maneuvers accompanied by palpable clicks is pathognomonic for reduction of radial head subluxation (nursemaid's elbow).
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Bilateral radial head subluxation (bilateral nursemaid's elbow)

CASE: M062
REASONING:

A 68-year-old female sustains a ground-level fall initially diagnosed on plain radiograph as an isolated left pubic symphysis fracture.

Persistent severe pain four days later prompts repeat X-ray and CT imaging, which reveals a previously missed (occult) concurrent left femoral neck fracture alongside mild joint space narrowing.

The diagnosis is further confirmed by definitive surgical management with a left hemiarthroplasty.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Concurrent (occult) left femoral neck fracture with left pubic symphysis fracture

CASE: M063
REASONING:

A 28-year-old primigravida undergoes prenatal ultrasound showing grade 3 fetal echogenic bowel (FEB) at 18 weeks and 3 days gestation, accompanied by progressive fetal bowel dilation reaching 21 mm by nearly 30 weeks.

The primary differential diagnosis for grade 3 FEB includes chromosomal aneuploidy (e.g., trisomy 21), congenital TORCH infections (especially CMV), and cystic fibrosis/meconium ileus (as well as intestinal atresia).

Normal first-trimester screening, low-risk NIPT, normal fetal echocardiogram, and negative maternal TORCH serologies effectively exclude chromosomal aneuploidies and congenital infections.

The combination of persistent grade 3 hyperechogenic bowel and progressive bowel dilation strongly points to inspissated meconium causing intestinal obstruction (meconium ileus), which is classically associated with cystic fibrosis.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Fetal meconium ileus (associated with cystic fibrosis)

CASE: M064
REASONING:

A 51-year-old HIV-negative, immunocompetent male from Palestine (Mediterranean/Middle Eastern origin) presents with a rapidly enlarging, painless purple nodule on the left forearm and hyperpigmented lesions on the back, without mucosal or visceral involvement on CT.

Normal immune cell subcounts and negative HIV, hepatitis B, and hepatitis C serologies exclude AIDS-related or iatrogenic immunodeficiency-associated disease.

Histopathology demonstrates strong positivity for CD31 (confirming a vascular/endothelial neoplasm) and HHV-8 (human herpesvirus-8), which is diagnostic of Kaposi sarcoma.

Given the patient's geographic background, age, HIV-negative status, and cutaneous-limited presentation, this represents the classic subtype of Kaposi sarcoma.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Classic Kaposi sarcoma
