CASE: M025
REASONING: This 39-year-old woman with obesity (BMI 30.14 kg/m²) presents with classic symptoms of increased intracranial pressure, including worsening peripheral visual field constriction, morning headaches, pulsatile tinnitus, and intermittent periocular pain. Her cerebrospinal fluid opening pressure is elevated at 27.2 cm H₂O with normal cellular and biochemical composition, and MRI/MRV rules out structural brain lesions, optic nerve abnormalities, and cerebral venous sinus thrombosis. Although elevated intraocular pressure and high myopia are present, her peripapillary RNFL thickness on OCT is normal, and fundus photography reveals no identifiable optic disc swelling (absence of papilledema). Fulfilling the diagnostic criteria for idiopathic intracranial hypertension in the absence of papilledema points directly to idiopathic intracranial hypertension without papilledema.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Idiopathic intracranial hypertension without papilledema (IIHWOP)

CASE: M026
REASONING: This 35-year-old male presented with nonspecific abdominal pain and an incidental renal lesion with a classic radiological discordance: homogeneous, hypodense, and non-enhancing on CT (Bosniak class 1), hyperechoic on ultrasound (typical of numerous microcystic acoustic interfaces), and showing internal nodular enhancement on MRI (Bosniak class 4). Histopathology demonstrates a tumor with multicystic architecture surrounded by fibrotic hypocellular stroma, with key mimics—MEST, AMLEC, ACD-RCC, and oncocytoma—explicitly excluded. This specific combination of male predominance, hypodense CT / hyperechoic ultrasound appearance, multicystic architecture with fibrotic hypocellular stroma, and oncocytic/cystic differential diagnosis is characteristic of tubulocystic renal cell carcinoma.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Tubulocystic renal cell carcinoma

CASE: M027
REASONING: This 25-year-old male without prior trauma, STIs, or instrumentation presents with a 6-month history of progressive obstructive lower urinary tract symptoms and a severely reduced maximum flow rate (4 mL/s). Imaging and urethrocystoscopy identify an annular stenosis in the bulbar urethra with a polypoid lesion located behind the stenotic segment. Complete endoscopic resection of the lesion restored normal voiding (maximum flow rate 28 mL/s). In a young adult male without inflammatory or traumatic risk factors, a benign obstructive polypoid lesion of the urethra confirmed on histology is most characteristically a urethral fibroepithelial polyp.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Urethral fibroepithelial polyp

CASE: M028
REASONING: This 48-year-old premenopausal woman presented with abnormal uterine bleeding, a large pelvic mass, markedly elevated CA-125 and CA 19-9, and surgical pathology showing endometrioid adenocarcinoma involving the endometrium (superficial myometrial invasion), right ovary (20 cm mass), and appendix. Although initially staged as synchronous primary ovarian and endometrial cancers, molecular profiling (NGS) demonstrated shared mutations (including an MSH6 hotspot mutation and MSI-H status without germline Lynch syndrome) and confirmed clonality between the two sites, establishing that the ovarian and extra-uterine disease represents metastasis from the primary endometrial tumor.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Metastatic endometrial endometrioid adenocarcinoma (endometrial endometrioid adenocarcinoma metastatic to the ovary and appendix)

CASE: M029
REASONING: This 29-year-old woman with a history of EBV-associated hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis underwent haploidentical HSCT and subsequently developed EBV-driven complications: first, CNS involvement with EBV DNA positivity in plasma, PBMCs, and CSF that responded to rituximab; and later, at 17 and 21 months post-transplant, widespread metabolically active subcutaneous masses on PET/CT. Biopsy confirmed EBV-positive NK/T cells in the subcutaneous lesions, and she achieved complete remission with an L-asparaginase-based regimen (L-GDP) plus a PD-1 inhibitor. The development of EBV-positive NK/T-cell lymphoid proliferation in the post-HSCT setting is diagnostic of EBV-positive NK/T-cell post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder (extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma-type PTLD).
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: EBV-positive NK/T-cell post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder (PTLD)

CASE: M030
REASONING: This 71-year-old male with advanced metastatic lung cancer presents with fever, asthenia, confusion, marked neutrophilic leukocytosis, and elevated CRP and procalcitonin, indicating acute bacterial sepsis. A key historical clue is a self-limiting five-day episode of diarrhea two months earlier, with no recent travel and a negative pleural fluid culture. Both blood cultures and a FilmArray gastrointestinal panel on stool tested positive for the same organism. Among the bacterial pathogens detected on the FilmArray GI panel, non-typhoidal Salmonella is uniquely classic for causing self-limiting gastroenteritis followed by prolonged stool carriage and subsequent bacteremia/sepsis in elderly or immunocompromised patients with solid malignancies.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Non-typhoidal Salmonella bacteremia

CASE: M031
REASONING: This 55-year-old female presented with progressive epigastric/periumbilical pain, nausea, and anorexia. Imaging revealed a large, necrotic peritoneal mass infiltrating adjacent fat and adhering to the stomach, pancreas, and transverse colon—features that strongly mimic a locally aggressive intra-abdominal malignancy—yet without distant metastasis. Surgical resection (partial gastrectomy and segmental colectomy) and pathology demonstrated a nonmalignant, well-vascularized mass with no recurrence at 1 year. A benign, locally adherent, well-vascularized peritoneal mass with necrosis that mimics malignancy and is cured by complete resection is most characteristic of an inflammatory myofibroblastic tumor.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumor (IMT)

CASE: M032
REASONING: Both female patients underwent venous sinus stenting (VSS) for transverse or transverse-sigmoid sinus stenosis (associated with idiopathic intracranial hypertension in Case 1 and pulsatile tinnitus in Case 2). Following initial intervention, both developed new or recurrent ipsilateral headaches, with new tinnitus and hearing changes in Case 1. Follow-up cerebral angiography in both cases identified a vascular lesion that was successfully obliterated using Onyx™ liquid embolic agent. A de novo dural arteriovenous fistula is a recognized vascular complication following venous sinus stenting that presents with headaches and pulsatile tinnitus and is definitively treated with Onyx™ embolization.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Dural arteriovenous fistula (dAVF)
