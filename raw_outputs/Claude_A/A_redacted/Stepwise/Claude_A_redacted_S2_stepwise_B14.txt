CASE: M105
REASONING: The patient has a slowly progressive focal-onset dystonia in the right leg, with sensory tricks and paradoxical improvement on backward walking. He also has horizontal nystagmus, subtle ataxia, impaired tandem gait and cerebellar atrophy on MRI. Cognition is intact (MoCA 30/30). The parents are first cousins and two siblings were affected, one with seizures and psychiatric illness and one who died at age 5. This points to an autosomal recessive neurodegenerative disorder, which fits the homozygous variant in the patient and the heterozygous carrier status of the unaffected sibling. Metabolic, Wilson (copper, ceruloplasmin), paraneoplastic and autoimmune workups are negative. The decisive finding is the skin biopsy: granular osmiophilic deposits (GRODs) on electron microscopy are the ultrastructural hallmark of neuronal ceroid lipofuscinosis, classically the PPT1/CLN1 type. Taken together, this is a late-onset NCL presenting atypically as progressive dystonia with cerebellar ataxia.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Adult-onset neuronal ceroid lipofuscinosis (likely CLN1/PPT1-related) presenting with progressive focal dystonia and cerebellar ataxia

CASE: M106
REASONING: A young man with no risk factors has a persistent inguinal mass and ipsilateral leg swelling. He was treated as proximal DVT for 6 months without improvement. Doppler showed a cystic 2.5 cm lesion at the common femoral vein with sluggish flow and no actual DVT, and CT venography showed the same. At surgery there was no extramural mass, so the "cystic lesion" arises from the vein itself and contains thrombus. A focal saccular or fusiform dilation of the common femoral vein, partly thrombosed, explains the palpable tender lump, the venous outflow obstruction, and the misdiagnosis as DVT.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Thrombosed common femoral vein aneurysm (primary venous aneurysm) causing venous outflow obstruction, mimicking DVT

CASE: M107
REASONING: This 3-year-old has cyanosis, failure to thrive, increased pulmonary vascularity, a single S2 and hepatomegaly, which suggest a mixing lesion with pulmonary overcirculation. Increased pulmonary vascularity argues against true pulmonary atresia with VSD. Echo, MSCT and catheterization agree on type I truncus arteriosus: a single truncal vessel with a short main pulmonary trunk, an overriding truncus, a regurgitant tricuspid truncal valve and a VSD. They also show the left coronary artery arising from the pulmonary trunk (ALCAPA), supported by lateral Q waves on ECG. Pulmonary vascular resistance is elevated and the VSD shunt is bidirectional because of delayed repair. Karyotyping is normal, although 22q11 deletion would need FISH or microarray to exclude.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Persistent truncus arteriosus type I (Collett-Edwards) with anomalous origin of the left coronary artery from the pulmonary trunk (ALCAPA) and pulmonary hypertension, previously misdiagnosed as pulmonary atresia with VSD

CASE: M108
REASONING: A 40-year-old has dry gangrene of the fingers despite normal arterial Doppler and normal peripheral pulses. Autoimmune and antiphospholipid workup is negative. Labs show marked leukocytosis (153,000/mm³), thrombocytosis (969,000/mm³), anemia and mild hepatosplenomegaly. The smear and marrow show chronic phase disease, and BCR-ABL is positive. Hyperleukocytosis with leukostasis and thrombocytosis-related microvascular occlusion explains digital ischemia with intact large-vessel flow. Smoking is an aggravating factor.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Chronic myeloid leukemia (BCR-ABL positive, chronic phase) presenting with digital gangrene due to hyperleukocytosis/thrombocytosis-related microvascular occlusion

CASE: M109
REASONING: She first presented with a unilateral follicular conjunctivitis treated as adenoviral (EKC) with topical steroid and antibiotic. Three weeks later she developed a conjunctival phlyctenule and then nodular anterior scleritis in the same eye. The systemic workup for autoimmune and infectious causes is negative except HSV-2 IgG positivity. Conjunctival PCR is negative for adenovirus, HSV-1 and HSV-2, which does not exclude herpetic immune-mediated disease. A unilateral follicular conjunctivitis progressing to phlyctenule and nodular scleritis after steroid exposure, with no other cause found, best fits HSV-associated inflammation.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Herpes simplex virus-associated anterior nodular scleritis (preceded by HSV follicular conjunctivitis/phlyctenule)

CASE: M110
REASONING: He has recurrent abrupt loss of consciousness with no prodrome and immediate recovery, which suggests cardiac (arrhythmic) syncope rather than seizure. Orthostatics, echo, CT and labs are normal, and EEG shows no epileptiform activity. ECG shows sinus bradycardia with sinus pauses, and telemetry captured an 8-second sinus pause at 37 bpm. EPS found no AVNRT or accessory pathway, and the dual AV node physiology is incidental. Heavy daily cannabis use (which raises vagal tone) and sleep deprivation are likely contributors.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Sinus node dysfunction (sick sinus syndrome) with symptomatic prolonged sinus pauses causing recurrent syncope, likely aggravated by chronic cannabis use

CASE: M111
REASONING: He has known MPO-ANCA vasculitis and is immunosuppressed. He presents with fever, neutrophilic leukocytosis and high CRP. The skin shows acute disseminated hemorrhagic, edematous, vesiculobullous and necrotic lesions mainly on the head, with mucosal erosions and a lung infiltrate. Biopsy shows a dense dermal neutrophilic infiltrate with Cryptococcus-like structures. These structures stain positive for MPO and negative on fungal stains and cultures, so they are degenerated neutrophils rather than organisms. Exhaustive infectious workup is negative. These findings define the cryptococcoid variant of Sweet syndrome, a bullous/necrotizing form. Extracutaneous involvement (pulmonary, mucosal) is consistent with it, and it occurs in the setting of ANCA-associated disease.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Cryptococcoid (bullous/necrotizing) Sweet syndrome (acute febrile neutrophilic dermatosis) with extracutaneous involvement, in a patient with MPO-ANCA vasculitis

CASE: M112
REASONING: A 65-year-old diabetic with prior stroke had repeated stereotyped transient left limb numbness and weakness (crescendo TIAs). These progressed to a persistent left hemiparesis with central facial and tongue palsy, left Babinski sign, sensory loss and severe right-sided headache. CTA shows 95% stenosis at the origin of the right ICA. DWI shows multiple scattered right hemispheric infarcts, consistent with artery-to-artery embolism and hypoperfusion. Asymmetrically prominent cortical veins on the right SWI reflect increased oxygen extraction in hypoperfused tissue, meaning misery perfusion and tissue at risk. His deficit worsened early (day 1). This is large-artery atherosclerotic stroke in the right ICA territory.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Acute progressive ischemic stroke in the right hemisphere due to severe (95%) atherosclerotic stenosis of the right internal carotid artery origin (large-artery atherosclerosis, embolic plus hypoperfusion mechanism), preceded by crescendo TIAs
