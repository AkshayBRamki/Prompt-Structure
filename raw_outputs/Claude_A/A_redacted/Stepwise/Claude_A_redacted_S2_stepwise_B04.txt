CASE: M025
REASONING: The patient has chronic peripheral field constriction that recently worsened, with morning headaches, pulsatile tinnitus and periocular pain. These are classic symptoms of raised intracranial pressure. Her BMI of 30 is a risk factor. CSF opening pressure is elevated at 27.2 cm H₂O, CSF composition is normal, and MRI/MRV is normal, with no mass, hydrocephalus or venous sinus thrombosis. Autoimmune panels are negative, so a secondary cause is unlikely. There is no papilledema, and OCT shows normal RNFL thickness. High myopia, with its tilted disc and peripapillary atrophy, can mask or prevent visible disc swelling. Normal ERG and macula exclude a retinal cause of the field loss. The mildly raised IOP does not explain the headache, tinnitus and CSF findings. Taken together, this is raised ICP without disc edema.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Idiopathic intracranial hypertension without papilledema (IIHWOP), with papilledema likely masked by high myopia

CASE: M026
REASONING: A young man has an incidentally found renal lesion. It looks benign-appearing on CT (Bosniak 1), but MRI shows internal nodular enhancement (Bosniak 4), which raises concern for a cystic neoplasm. Histology shows a multicystic tumor separated by fibrotic, hypocellular stroma. The pathologists explicitly excluded the main mimics of a multicystic renal tumor: MEST/cystic nephroma (which would have ovarian-type stroma), angiomyolipoma with epithelial cysts, acquired cystic disease-associated RCC (no end-stage kidney disease), and oncocytoma. Tubulocystic RCC fits best. It shows a strong male predominance, is made of tubules and cysts within fibrotic hypocellular stroma, and is classically Bosniak III/IV on MRI.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Tubulocystic renal cell carcinoma

CASE: M027
REASONING: A 25-year-old man has progressive obstructive voiding symptoms and a very low Qmax of 4 mL/s. He has no history of trauma, STI or instrumentation. Retrograde urethrography and cystoscopy show a short annular bulbar stricture with a polypoid lesion just proximal to it. In a young man with no risk factors, an annular bulbar narrowing suggests a congenital membrane-type stricture. The associated polypoid lesion was resected and confirmed on histology. A benign urethral polyp is the most likely lesion, and flow returned to normal (28 mL/s) after treatment.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Fibroepithelial polyp of the bulbar urethra associated with an annular (congenital-type) bulbar urethral stricture

CASE: M028
REASONING: The patient has a large adnexal mass with peritoneal and appendiceal spread, very high CA-125 and CA 19-9, and abnormal uterine bleeding. Pathology initially suggested two synchronous primaries: an ovarian endometrioid carcinoma and a stage IA endometrial endometrioid carcinoma. NGS, however, showed shared mutations, including the MSH6 hotspot and MSI-H status, which proves the two tumors are clonally related. The ovarian tumor shows higher tumor mutation burden, consistent with later evolution. Germline MMR testing was negative, so the MSI-H is somatic rather than Lynch syndrome. The ovarian and appendiceal disease are therefore metastases from a single uterine primary.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Primary MSI-high (somatic MSH6-mutated) endometrial endometrioid adenocarcinoma with metastasis to the ovary and appendix (advanced stage), not synchronous independent primaries

CASE: M029
REASONING: EBV-driven HLH was treated with DEP chemotherapy and then haploidentical HSCT, with full donor chimerism. At 11 months she developed spinal cord swelling with EBV DNA in plasma, PBMCs and CSF that responded to rituximab, consistent with CNS EBV-associated post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder. At 17 and 21 months she had subcutaneous masses containing EBV-positive NK/T cells. Given full donor chimerism, this is a donor-derived, EBV-driven NK/T-cell lymphoma arising in the post-transplant setting, a rare PTLD subtype. It was treated with an asparaginase-based regimen (L-GDP) plus a PD-1 inhibitor, which is standard for extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: EBV-associated post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder: CNS (spinal) EBV-PTLD followed by EBV-positive extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma (cutaneous/subcutaneous) after haplo-HSCT for EBV-HLH

CASE: M030
REASONING: An elderly, immunocompromised man with metastatic lung cancer presents with fever, confusion and marked neutrophilic leukocytosis. Procalcitonin is elevated. He had a self-limited diarrheal illness two months earlier. The same organism was found in blood cultures and on the FilmArray stool panel, which points to an enteric pathogen that invades the bloodstream. Older, immunocompromised patients are prone to this, and their gastroenteritis may be followed by bacteremia or prolonged carriage. The pleural culture was negative, so the drainage is not the source. Non-typhoidal Salmonella is the most common enteric pathogen causing bacteremia in this setting.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Non-typhoidal Salmonella enterica bacteremia secondary to gastrointestinal carriage/infection in an immunocompromised host

CASE: M031
REASONING: A middle-aged woman has a large, partly necrotic intraperitoneal mass in the perigastric region. It infiltrates adjacent fat and adheres to the stomach, pancreas and transverse colon, with no metastases. Intraoperative pathology called it nonmalignant, but it was locally aggressive enough to need en bloc colectomy and partial gastrectomy. A benign but locally infiltrative intra-abdominal mesenchymal tumor that does not metastasize best fits desmoid-type fibromatosis. Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumor is the main alternative. There was no recurrence at one year.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Intra-abdominal (mesenteric/omental) desmoid-type fibromatosis (aggressive fibromatosis)

CASE: M032
REASONING: Both patients had IIH-related transverse/sigmoid sinus stenosis treated with venous sinus stenting. Afterwards, both developed new or recurrent pulsatile tinnitus and headaches ipsilateral to the treated sinus. Follow-up angiography showed a lesion that was obliterated with Onyx embolization. A new arteriovenous shunt adjacent to the stented sinus is a recognized complication of venous sinus stenting. It explains the pulsatile tinnitus and is treated with Onyx.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Dural arteriovenous fistula of the transverse–sigmoid sinus arising after venous sinus stenting (de novo post-VSS dAVF)
