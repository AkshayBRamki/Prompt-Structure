CASE: M097

KEY FINDINGS:
Late-onset dementia with a mixed movement disorder: akathisia, axial/somatic dyskinesia of the diaphragm and abdomen, and parkinsonism. This points to widespread cortical and basal ganglia involvement.
Psychosis with confabulation, a complex mood disorder and sleep disorder. This is a broad neuropsychiatric picture that fits poorly within one classic neurodegenerative syndrome.
CT shows marked cerebral atrophy and slight pallidal calcifications. The calcifications are likely nonspecific at age 83.
Normal HTT CAG repeats exclude Huntington disease.
Microbiome sequencing shows Spirochaetes dominated by Treponema (T. medium, T. denticola, others). These oral treponemes are implicated in spirochete-associated neurodegeneration and can cause a neurosyphilis-like picture.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Spirochetal (oral Treponema-associated) dementia with movement disorder
Dementia with Lewy bodies
Neurosyphilis (T. pallidum)
Huntington disease / HD-like phenocopy
Primary familial brain calcification (Fahr)
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Treponemal dementia.
For: Treponema-dominant spirochete signal; dementia with psychosis, confabulation and mixed hyper- and hypokinetic features resembling general paresis; marked atrophy.
Against: this is microbiome evidence, not CSF or tissue proof of CNS infection.
Dementia with Lewy bodies.
For: the initial diagnosis; parkinsonism, psychosis and sleep disorder.
Against: prominent diaphragmatic and abdominal dyskinesia and akathisia are atypical, and the spirochete finding offers an alternative explanation.
Neurosyphilis (T. pallidum).
For: the phenotype resembles general paresis.
Against: the identified species are oral treponemes, not T. pallidum.
Huntington disease.
For: chorea-like dyskinesia, dementia and psychosis.
Against: normal CAG repeats and no family history.
Fahr.
For: pallidal calcification.
Against: the calcification is only slight, onset is very late, and there is no family history.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Huntington disease is excluded genetically.
Fahr is excluded because the calcification is too mild and nonspecific.
Classic T. pallidum neurosyphilis is unsupported by the species profile.
Lewy body dementia does not explain the atypical dyskinesias and is superseded by the Treponema finding.
I commit to spirochetal dementia.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Spirochetal (oral Treponema-associated) dementia with late-onset dyskinesia and parkinsonism, mimicking Lewy body dementia.

CASE: M098

KEY FINDINGS:
Hurley stage III hidradenitis suppurativa (the redacted term) with active draining sinus tracts despite adalimumab.
Systemic features: fever, weight loss, malaise, leukocytosis and neutrophilia, and hypercalcemia. These indicate a heavy systemic inflammatory burden.
Marked polyclonal IgG elevation, including IgG4, with sialadenitis and jaw swelling. This raises the question of IgG4-related disease.
Biopsy shows fewer than 5% IgG4-positive cells, which excludes IgG4-RD histologically.
PET shows hypermetabolic cutaneous inflammation consistent with the flare. The monospot is positive, but no infection was confirmed.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Severe hidradenitis suppurativa with systemic inflammation, polyclonal hypergammaglobulinemia (IgG4 elevation as a mimic) and sialadenitis
IgG4-related disease
Sarcoidosis
EBV / infectious mononucleosis sialadenitis
Lymphoproliferative disorder
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Severe HS with systemic inflammation.
For: uncontrolled stage III disease with hypermetabolic lesions; a severe HS burden explains the high IgG, neutrophilia and constitutional symptoms.
Against: sialadenitis is unusual in HS.
IgG4-RD.
For: high IgG4 and salivary gland involvement.
Against: fewer than 5% IgG4-positive cells and no storiform fibrosis or obliterative phlebitis.
Sarcoidosis.
For: hypercalcemia and parotid involvement.
Against: no granulomas on biopsy.
EBV.
For: positive monospot.
Against: a year-long course and no confirmed infectious pathology.
Lymphoma.
For: constitutional symptoms and lymphadenopathy.
Against: lymph node biopsy shows no neoplasm.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
IgG4-RD is excluded on histology.
Sarcoidosis and lymphoma are excluded because the biopsy lacks granulomas or malignancy.
EBV is not substantiated.
I commit to systemic inflammation driven by severe HS.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Severe (Hurley III) hidradenitis suppurativa flare with systemic inflammation: polyclonal hypergammaglobulinemia with elevated IgG4 mimicking IgG4-related disease, sialadenitis, hypercalcemia and leukocytosis.

CASE: M099

KEY FINDINGS:
Subacute, symmetric proximal weakness with myalgia during statin therapy.
Markedly elevated CK and a myopathic EMG showing myonecrosis.
Biopsy shows necrotic and regenerating fibers with scarce inflammation, the pattern of necrotizing myopathy.
Anti-HMGCR is strongly positive (>200 CU). SRP is negative, thyroid function is normal, and colonoscopy is normal.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Statin-associated anti-HMGCR immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM)
Statin toxic myopathy, self-limited
Anti-SRP IMNM
Polymyositis / paraneoplastic myositis
Hypothyroid myopathy
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Anti-HMGCR IMNM.
For: statin exposure, very high CK, necrosis without inflammation, and strongly positive anti-HMGCR.
Against: nothing significant.
Toxic statin myopathy.
For: statin use.
Against: progression despite a low dose, a high antibody titer, and a necrotizing biopsy.
Anti-SRP IMNM.
For: necrotizing histology.
Against: SRP is negative.
Polymyositis / paraneoplastic myositis.
For: proximal weakness.
Against: scarce inflammation on biopsy and a normal colonoscopy.
Hypothyroid myopathy.
For: proximal weakness with raised CK.
Against: normal thyroid function.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Hypothyroid myopathy, anti-SRP IMNM and polymyositis are excluded by the tests above.
Toxic statin myopathy does not fit the antibody result or the persistent course.
I commit to anti-HMGCR IMNM.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Statin-associated anti-HMGCR immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy.

CASE: M100

KEY FINDINGS:
A previously normal toddler with prolonged unilateral (left-sided) focal seizures at age 2.
Persistent spastic left hemiparesis after the seizures, followed by developmental regression.
MRI shows right hemispheric atrophy, ipsilateral ventricular dilatation and white matter gliosis.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Hemiconvulsion–hemiplegia(–epilepsy) syndrome (HH/HHE)
Dyke–Davidoff–Masson syndrome
Rasmussen encephalitis
Sturge–Weber syndrome
Perinatal or arterial ischemic stroke
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
HH(E).
For: the classic sequence of prolonged unilateral seizure at under 4 years, followed by lasting hemiplegia and hemiatrophy.
Against: later epilepsy is not yet documented.
Dyke–Davidoff–Masson.
For: cerebral hemiatrophy.
Against: no calvarial thickening or sinus hyperpneumatization is described. It is also a largely radiological label for the same end state.
Rasmussen encephalitis.
For: unilateral atrophy and focal seizures.
Against: no epilepsia partialis continua and no ongoing progressive seizures; onset was a single acute prolonged event.
Sturge–Weber.
For: hemiatrophy.
Against: no port-wine stain or leptomeningeal enhancement.
Stroke.
For: hemiparesis.
Against: normal development before the seizures, and diffuse hemispheric atrophy rather than a vascular-territory infarct.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Sturge–Weber and stroke are excluded on the features above.
Rasmussen encephalitis does not fit the temporal pattern.
Dyke–Davidoff–Masson describes the imaging consequence but lacks its skull signs.
I commit to HH syndrome.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Hemiconvulsion–hemiplegia(–epilepsy) syndrome with right cerebral hemiatrophy.

CASE: M101

KEY FINDINGS:
A child with raised intracranial pressure (headache worse on straining, vomiting) and ataxia.
Bradycardia with hypertension suggests a Cushing response.
A 4 cm vermian / fourth-ventricle-roof mass with obstructive hydrocephalus.
The mass shows heterogeneous enhancement, T1 hypointensity and restricted diffusion with low ADC, indicating a highly cellular tumor. SWI blooming indicates hemorrhage or calcification.
Craniovertebral junction anomalies (atlanto-occipital assimilation, basilar invagination) are incidental.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Medulloblastoma
Ependymoma
Pilocytic astrocytoma
Atypical teratoid/rhabdoid tumor (AT/RT)
Hemangioblastoma
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Medulloblastoma.
For: vermian and fourth-ventricle-roof origin, marked diffusion restriction, heterogeneous enhancement, and pediatric age.
Against: age 13 is older than the peak, and SWI blooming is less typical.
Ependymoma.
For: fourth ventricle location and frequent calcification or blooming.
Against: it usually arises from the floor and extends through the foramina, and diffusion restriction is usually less marked.
Pilocytic astrocytoma.
For: cerebellar location in a child.
Against: it is typically cystic with a mural nodule and does not restrict diffusion.
AT/RT.
For: restricted diffusion with hemorrhage.
Against: it usually occurs before age 3.
Hemangioblastoma.
For: posterior fossa location.
Against: rare in children, and typically cyst plus nodule without restriction.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Pilocytic astrocytoma, hemangioblastoma and AT/RT are excluded on imaging pattern and age.
Ependymoma is less likely because of the roof origin and strong restriction.
I commit to medulloblastoma.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Medulloblastoma of the vermis / fourth ventricle roof with obstructive hydrocephalus, plus coincident craniovertebral junction anomalies (basilar invagination, atlanto-occipital assimilation).

CASE: M102

KEY FINDINGS:
A previously healthy child with a febrile prodrome that progressed to cardiogenic shock (hypotension, oliguria, confusion, gallop rhythm, rales).
Raised troponin, CK-MB and BNP.
ECG shows conduction disease (RBBB, wide QRS) and premature ventricular contractions.
Ejection fraction is 28% without ventricular dilatation, which points to acute rather than chronic cardiomyopathy.
HRV-A66 was detected by both mNGS and PCR, and blood cultures are negative.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Fulminant viral myocarditis due to human rhinovirus A66
MIS-C
Septic cardiomyopathy
Dilated cardiomyopathy
Kawasaki disease with myocarditis
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
HRV-A66 myocarditis.
For: viral prodrome, acute severe dysfunction without dilatation, conduction disease, biomarker rise, and a confirmed virus.
Against: rhinovirus is an uncommon cause of myocarditis.
MIS-C.
For: fever, gastrointestinal symptoms and shock.
Against: no SARS-CoV-2 link, no rash or conjunctivitis, and an alternative virus was found.
Septic cardiomyopathy.
For: shock.
Against: negative cultures.
Dilated cardiomyopathy.
For: low ejection fraction.
Against: no ventricular enlargement and an acute onset.
Kawasaki disease.
For: fever and myocardial involvement.
Against: none of the mucocutaneous criteria.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Sepsis, dilated cardiomyopathy and Kawasaki disease are excluded on the findings above.
MIS-C is unsupported.
I commit to viral fulminant myocarditis.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Fulminant myocarditis caused by human rhinovirus A66, with cardiogenic shock.

CASE: M103

KEY FINDINGS:
Recent abdominal trauma with lumbar fracture, followed by a large abdomino-pelvic cystic mass.
MRI shows T1/T2 hyperintensity (blood products) with a dark T2 peripheral rim (hemosiderin or fibrous capsule).
The ovaries are normal, tumor markers are normal, and anemia is mild.
Histology shows a fibrous hyalinized wall without epithelial lining, containing necrotic-hemorrhagic content and clots, and no neoplasm.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Post-traumatic encapsulated hematoma (chronic expanding hematoma / hemorrhagic peritoneal pseudocyst)
Tuberculous cold abscess or peritoneal tuberculosis
Cystic lymphangioma / mesenteric cyst
Ovarian endometrioma or cystic ovarian neoplasm
Cystic mesothelioma
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Post-traumatic hematoma.
For: trauma history, blood-signal MRI with a hemosiderin rim, and a fibrous pseudocapsule without lining on histology.
Against: nothing significant.
Tuberculosis.
For: prior pulmonary TB.
Against: no granulomas or caseation.
Lymphangioma / mesenteric cyst.
For: cystic peritoneal mass.
Against: no endothelial or epithelial lining, and the content is hemorrhagic.
Endometrioma / ovarian neoplasm.
For: T1-bright cyst in a young woman.
Against: normal ovaries and no endometrial stroma.
Cystic mesothelioma.
For: peritoneal cyst.
Against: no mesothelial lining or neoplastic component.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
The neoplastic and lined cysts are excluded histologically.
Tuberculosis is excluded by the absence of granulomas.
I commit to a post-traumatic encapsulated hematoma.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Post-traumatic chronic encapsulated (expanding) peritoneal hematoma, i.e. a hemorrhagic pseudocyst.

CASE: M104

KEY FINDINGS:
A 7-year-old boy with 6 months of progressive visual, auditory, cognitive and motor decline, now with status-like seizures.
Spastic quadriparesis with pseudobulbar affect.
Diffuse hyperpigmentation with high ACTH, indicating primary adrenal insufficiency.
Parieto-occipital white matter disease (Loes 22) and elevated very long chain fatty acids (C24, C26 and their ratios).
Normal lactate and negative Krabbe markers.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Childhood cerebral X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy with Addison disease
Metachromatic leukodystrophy
Krabbe disease
Mitochondrial leukoencephalopathy
Other peroxisomal disorder (e.g. single-enzyme defects)
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
X-ALD.
For: male sex, typical age, posterior white matter pattern, very high VLCFA, and adrenal insufficiency.
Against: no genetic confirmation.
Metachromatic leukodystrophy.
For: leukodystrophy with regression.
Against: no adrenal failure, and VLCFA are not elevated in MLD.
Krabbe disease.
For: leukodystrophy.
Against: negative markers.
Mitochondrial disorder.
For: multisystem neurological decline.
Against: normal lactate.
Other peroxisomal defect.
For: elevated VLCFA.
Against: these usually present in infancy with dysmorphism and liver disease.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Krabbe disease and mitochondrial disease are excluded by the tests above.
MLD and other peroxisomal disorders fit poorly with VLCFA elevation plus Addison disease at this age.
I commit to X-ALD.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Childhood cerebral X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy with primary adrenal insufficiency (Addison disease)
