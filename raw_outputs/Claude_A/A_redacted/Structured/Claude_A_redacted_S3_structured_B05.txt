CASE: M033

KEY FINDINGS:
Severe neuropathic pain (stabbing, shock-like, burning) starting about 21 days after dengue. This latency points to a post-infectious, immune-mediated process.
Proximal-predominant amyotrophy and paresis of the right arm (deltoid, infraspinatus, biceps). This matches upper and middle trunk (C5–C7) territory.
The right upper limb shows allodynia, thermal hyperesthesia, deep hypoesthesia, absent reflexes and hypotonia. This is a lower motor neuron and sensory pattern confined to one limb.
ENMG shows acute denervation with increased latency, which means peripheral nerve or plexus injury.
MRI shows signal change in the upper and middle trunks of the brachial plexus. This localizes the lesion to the plexus.
Dengue IgM is positive and other inflammatory, infectious and immune work-up is normal, which supports dengue as the trigger.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Neuralgic amyotrophy (Parsonage-Turner syndrome) triggered by dengue
Guillain-Barré syndrome after dengue
Cervical radiculopathy (C5–C6)
Complex regional pain syndrome
Dengue-associated myositis
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Parsonage-Turner syndrome:
For: severe pain followed by weakness and atrophy, post-viral latency, one limb, proximal muscles, plexus changes on MRI, denervation on ENMG.
Against: nothing significant.
Guillain-Barré syndrome:
For: post-infectious timing, areflexia.
Against: only one limb is affected, there is no ascending or symmetric weakness, and severe focal pain is atypical.
Cervical radiculopathy:
For: C5–C6 distribution.
Against: lesion is in the plexus trunks on MRI, onset was acute after infection, and no spinal cause is described.
CRPS:
For: allodynia, livedo, burning pain.
Against: objective denervation, areflexia and plexus lesion show true nerve injury.
Myositis:
For: post-viral weakness.
Against: neuropathic pain, sensory loss, areflexia and an ENMG pattern of denervation rather than myopathy.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
GBS is excluded because the deficit is unilateral and focal.
Radiculopathy is excluded because MRI localizes to the plexus.
CRPS and myositis are excluded because of the objective neurogenic findings.
I commit to dengue-triggered neuralgic amyotrophy.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Parsonage-Turner syndrome (neuralgic amyotrophy / acute brachial plexus neuritis) of the right upper and middle trunks after dengue infection.

CASE: M034

KEY FINDINGS:
Case 1:
Right facial palsy of the lower motor neuron type (forehead involved), progressing slowly over one year.
Right sensorineural hearing loss and impaired corneal reflex, indicating involvement of cranial nerves V, VII and VIII at the cerebellopontine (CP) angle.
MRI shows a lesion with CSF signal at the right CP angle abutting cranial nerves VII and VIII and the AICA.
Case 2:
Recurrent "Bell's palsy" with residual weakness, which is a red flag for a structural cause.
MRI shows an AICA vascular loop compressing cranial nerves VII and VIII.
Case 3:
Bilateral facial palsy with involvement of cranial nerves IX and X, plus fever.
IgM ELISA and western blot were positive, which is the two-tier serology for Borrelia.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Case 1:
Arachnoid cyst of the CP angle
Epidermoid cyst
Vestibular schwannoma
Facial schwannoma
Case 2:
Neurovascular compression by an AICA loop
Recurrent idiopathic Bell's palsy
CP angle tumor
Case 3:
Lyme neuroborreliosis
Neurosarcoidosis
Guillain-Barré syndrome variant
Carcinomatous or lymphomatous meningitis
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Case 1:
Arachnoid cyst:
For: signal identical to CSF, slow progression, displaces rather than invades nerves.
Against: none.
Epidermoid:
For: CP angle location, near-CSF signal.
Against: it is typically lobulated and encases nerves and vessels; no diffusion restriction is described.
Schwannomas:
For: involvement of cranial nerves VII and VIII.
Against: they are solid enhancing lesions, not CSF-intensity.
Case 2:
AICA loop:
For: directly shown compressing cranial nerves VII and VIII, recurrent palsy.
Against: none.
Idiopathic Bell's palsy:
For: abrupt onset.
Against: recurrence, incomplete recovery and a compressive lesion seen on imaging.
Tumor:
Against: no mass seen.
Case 3:
Lyme disease:
For: bilateral facial palsy, multiple cranial nerves, fever, positive IgM ELISA confirmed by western blot.
Against: no tick exposure was recalled, but this is often absent.
Sarcoidosis:
For: bilateral facial palsy.
Against: serum ACE work-up did not confirm it and serology is positive for Borrelia.
GBS:
For: cranial neuropathies.
Against: no limb weakness, fever is present, and serology is positive.
Malignant meningitis:
Against: serology explains the picture.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Case 1: schwannomas are excluded because the lesion has CSF signal, and an epidermoid is less likely given the morphology. I commit to arachnoid cyst.
Case 2: the vascular loop is the demonstrated cause.
Case 3: two-tier serology confirms Lyme disease. Sarcoidosis and GBS are excluded.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS:
Case 1: right CP angle arachnoid cyst causing facial palsy.
Case 2: AICA vascular loop compression causing recurrent facial palsy.
Case 3: Lyme neuroborreliosis with bilateral facial and multiple cranial neuropathies.
Overall theme: secondary, non-idiopathic causes of facial nerve palsy.

CASE: M035

KEY FINDINGS:
Enthesitis at several sites on MRI (spinous processes, ischial tuberosity, trochanters, greater tuberosity of the humerus). This is the hallmark of a spondyloarthritis-type process.
No sacroiliitis, and HLA-B27 is negative.
Positive Chlamydia trachomatis IgA and IgG, together with pyuria and high sexual activity. This indicates a recent or ongoing genitourinary infection.
Raised WBC and CRP, indicating systemic inflammation.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Reactive arthritis/enthesitis associated with Chlamydia
Axial spondyloarthritis or ankylosing spondylitis
Polymyalgia rheumatica
DISH or mechanical enthesopathy
Septic arthritis or gonococcal infection
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Reactive arthritis:
For: urogenital infection with positive Chlamydia IgA, inflammatory enthesitis at several sites, raised CRP.
Against: HLA-B27 negative, but this does not exclude it.
Axial SpA:
For: enthesitis, back pain.
Against: no sacroiliitis, HLA-B27 negative, and there is a clear infectious trigger.
PMR:
For: shoulder and hip girdle pain, raised CRP.
Against: age in the 50s is borderline, and the pattern is enthesitis with bone marrow edema rather than bursitis alone.
DISH or mechanical enthesopathy:
Against: inflammation on MRI and raised markers.
Septic arthritis or gonococcal infection:
Against: no joint effusion described, and the process involves several sites with tendon insertions rather than a purulent joint.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Axial SpA is excluded because there is no sacroiliitis and there is a clear trigger.
PMR, DISH and septic causes are excluded on the imaging pattern.
I commit to Chlamydia-induced reactive arthritis presenting mainly as enthesitis.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Reactive arthritis/enthesitis (HLA-B27-negative) after Chlamydia trachomatis urogenital infection.

CASE: M036

KEY FINDINGS:
Young nulliparous woman with a large unilateral adnexal mass (9.4 cm).
AFP is markedly raised (>60,500 ng/mL), which is the marker of yolk sac elements. LDH is also raised.
CA-125 is only mildly raised, which is nonspecific and possibly related to her endometriosis.
Histology shows microcystic and endodermal sinus patterns with Schiller-Duval bodies, and AFP immunohistochemistry is positive. These findings are diagnostic.
She has a history of endometriosis, which is a confounder.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Ovarian yolk sac tumor (endodermal sinus tumor)
Mixed malignant germ cell tumor
Endometriosis-associated clear cell or endometrioid carcinoma (including somatically derived yolk sac differentiation)
Immature teratoma
Endometrioma, possibly infected
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Yolk sac tumor:
For: age, very high AFP, Schiller-Duval bodies, AFP-positive staining, typical histologic patterns.
Against: none.
Mixed germ cell tumor:
For: possible in this age group.
Against: no other germ cell components are described.
Clear cell or endometrioid carcinoma:
For: background endometriosis, CA-125.
Against: age, AFP level and pure yolk sac morphology.
Immature teratoma:
Against: no neuroepithelium, and AFP is far too high.
Endometrioma:
Against: solid malignant histology and tumor markers.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Histology and AFP exclude the epithelial and benign options.
There is no evidence of other germ cell elements.
I commit to a pure yolk sac tumor.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Ovarian yolk sac tumor (endodermal sinus tumor) of the left ovary, FIGO stage IA.

CASE: M037

KEY FINDINGS:
Elderly woman living in an endemic area of Brazil.
Fungal spores in the appendix with acute appendicitis.
Generalized lymphadenopathy, plus nodes at the hepatic hilum and in the retroperitoneum causing obstructive jaundice.
Adrenal nodules (adrenal glands are a classic target).
Weight loss of 20 kg.
Lungs are normal, TB test is negative, and liver biopsy is negative for cancer.
E. coli bacteremia, which is secondary to gut and biliary involvement.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Disseminated paracoccidioidomycosis (acute/subacute "juvenile-type" lymphoabdominal form)
Disseminated histoplasmosis
Abdominal tuberculosis
Lymphoma
Metastatic carcinoma (e.g., gallbladder)
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Paracoccidioidomycosis:
For: endemic in Brazil, fungal elements in the appendix, lymphoabdominal and lymph node pattern, adrenal involvement, wasting.
Against: her age and normal lungs are atypical for the chronic adult form, but they fit the acute/subacute form.
Histoplasmosis:
For: dissemination, adrenal involvement, lymphadenopathy.
Against: the endemic context and the fungal morphology attributed to the redacted organism.
Tuberculosis:
For: abdominal nodes, adrenal involvement, weight loss.
Against: negative TB test, and fungi were identified.
Lymphoma:
For: widespread lymphadenopathy, weight loss.
Against: no neoplastic pattern in the nodes.
Metastatic carcinoma:
For: gallbladder thickening, adrenal nodules.
Against: liver biopsy negative, and a fungal cause was demonstrated.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Malignancy is excluded by the negative biopsies and node findings.
TB is excluded by the negative test.
Histoplasmosis is less consistent with the endemic setting and histology.
I commit to paracoccidioidomycosis.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Disseminated paracoccidioidomycosis (Paracoccidioides brasiliensis) with appendiceal, lymph node, biliary and adrenal involvement, presenting as appendicitis.

CASE: M038

KEY FINDINGS:
Schimmelpenning syndrome, a mosaic RASopathy (HRAS/KRAS) with an epidermal and sebaceous nevus.
Progressive headache and dizziness, indicating raised intracranial pressure.
A large irregular left frontal mass (6.5 cm) with cystic components and marked mass effect.
HRAS p.G13R mutation at a 31% allele fraction. This is a RAS-pathway driver shared with the mosaic disorder, linking the tumor to the syndrome.
DIFFERENTIAL:
High-grade glioma (glioblastoma)
Lower-grade glioma (astrocytoma or pleomorphic xanthoastrocytoma)
Ganglioglioma or other glioneuronal tumor
Brain metastasis or abscess
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Glioblastoma:
For: age, large irregular cystic or necrotic mass with major mass effect, progressive symptoms, RAS-pathway activation.
Against: none of the specific histologic grading features are given.
Lower-grade glioma or PXA:
For: cystic component, MAPK-pathway mutation.
Against: irregular and aggressive features in a 44-year-old.
Ganglioglioma:
For: cystic, MAPK-driven.
Against: age, size, no seizure history.
Metastasis or abscess:
Against: no primary cancer or infection, and the mutation is shared with the patient's mosaic syndrome.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Metastasis and abscess are excluded.
The imaging behavior favors a high-grade glial tumor.
I commit to a high-grade glioma carrying the mosaic HRAS mutation. This is moderately confident, since histologic grading is not given.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Glioblastoma (high-grade glioma) of the left frontal lobe, HRAS G13R-mutated, associated with Schimmelpenning syndrome.

CASE: M039

KEY FINDINGS:
Adolescent non-smoker with hemoptysis, cough and dyspnea.
A polypoid tumor inside the left main bronchus, with a calcified left upper lobe mass.
Widespread metastases (right lung, spine, pelvis), meaning a malignant disease.
Malignant spindle cells that are cytokeratin+, vimentin+ and TTF-1+. These indicate a pulmonary epithelial origin with sarcomatoid (mesenchymal-like) differentiation.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Pulmonary blastoma / pulmonary sarcomatoid carcinoma
Synovial sarcoma (primary pulmonary or metastatic)
Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumor
Pleuropulmonary blastoma
Carcinoid tumor
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Pulmonary blastoma / sarcomatoid carcinoma:
For: CK+/TTF-1+ spindle cells, vimentin co-expression, endobronchial mass, aggressive metastatic behavior, can occur in young non-smokers.
Against: rare in adolescents.
Synovial sarcoma:
For: young age, CK+/vimentin+ spindle cells.
Against: TTF-1 positivity favors a lung epithelial origin, and no translocation was tested.
Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumor:
For: common endobronchial tumor in adolescents.
Against: widespread metastases, TTF-1+, overtly malignant cells.
Pleuropulmonary blastoma:
Against: occurs in children under 6, is cytokeratin-negative, and is often cystic.
Carcinoid:
For: endobronchial, young patient, hemoptysis.
Against: spindle-cell malignancy, and vimentin expression with sarcomatoid features.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumor, pleuropulmonary blastoma and carcinoid are excluded on age, behavior and immunoprofile.
Synovial sarcoma is less likely given TTF-1.
I commit to a primary lung sarcomatoid carcinoma of the blastoma type.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Pulmonary blastoma (pulmonary sarcomatoid carcinoma), metastatic to the right lung, vertebrae and pelvis.

CASE: M040

KEY FINDINGS:
Teenager with an ischemic stroke in the left middle cerebral artery (MCA) territory and no vascular risk factors. Normal labs and coagulation studies argue against a clotting disorder.
Narrow left carotid canal (3 mm vs 7 mm) on bone CT. A small bony canal shows the artery was small from development, not narrowed later.
Hypoplastic left internal carotid artery (ICA) with poor filling of M1 and M2 and sparse cortical branches.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Congenital hypoplasia of the left ICA causing hemodynamic or embolic stroke
Moyamoya disease
Carotid dissection
Focal cerebral arteriopathy or vasculitis
Cardioembolic stroke
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
ICA hypoplasia:
For: one small ICA within a small bony canal, a downstream MCA with poor filling.
Against: none.
Moyamoya:
For: steno-occlusive arteriopathy in a young patient.
Against: no basal collateral network is described, and the narrow bony canal shows a developmental, not progressive, cause.
Dissection:
For: young stroke.
Against: a dissection does not reshape bone, and no flap or hematoma is described.
Focal arteriopathy or vasculitis:
For: pediatric MCA stroke.
Against: the bony canal is abnormal, and inflammatory and other labs are normal.
Cardioembolism:
Against: normal cardiopulmonary exam and a structural carotid anomaly that explains the stroke.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
The narrow carotid canal is the deciding finding, and it excludes acquired narrowing (moyamoya, dissection, vasculitis).
Cardioembolism is unsupported.
I commit to congenital ICA hypoplasia.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Congenital hypoplasia of the left internal carotid artery with ischemic stroke in the left MCA territory.
