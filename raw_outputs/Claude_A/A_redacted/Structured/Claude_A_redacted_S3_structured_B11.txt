CASE: M081

KEY FINDINGS:
Bilateral symmetric calcifications of the basal ganglia, thalami and subcortical white matter on CT. This is the defining imaging finding.
Progressive neuropsychiatric decline: agitation, dysarthria, seizures, chorea, rigidity, spasticity, cognitive impairment (MoCA 22/30). This is the typical clinical picture of striatopallidodentate calcification.
Copper and calcium metabolism tests were normal, which excludes Wilson disease and the parathyroid causes of calcification.
Anemia, high ESR and high CRP point to chronic inflammation or deprivation, but nothing in them is specific.
Subclinical hypothyroidism (TSH 13.92) is an incidental finding and does not explain the calcification.
Onset is in adolescence with no family history, so the condition is sporadic or not yet recognized as familial.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Fahr disease (primary/idiopathic basal ganglia calcification)
Secondary Fahr syndrome from hypoparathyroidism or pseudohypoparathyroidism
Wilson disease
Mitochondrial disorder (e.g., MELAS, Kearns-Sayre)
Post-infectious or inflammatory calcification (e.g., neurocysticercosis, TORCH sequelae)
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Fahr disease
For: symmetric calcification in a classic distribution; mixed movement disorder; seizures; cognitive decline; metabolic causes excluded.
Against: no family history (although sporadic cases occur); onset is earlier than the typical 4th to 5th decade.
Hypoparathyroidism or pseudohypoparathyroidism
For: this is the most common cause of the same imaging picture.
Against: calcium metabolism was normal and the case states it was ruled out.
Wilson disease
For: adolescent onset; chorea, rigidity and dysarthria; dark urine early in the illness.
Against: copper studies ruled it out; Wilson disease does not cause calcification.
Mitochondrial disorder
For: seizures, headaches, cognitive decline, basal ganglia calcification.
Against: no stroke-like episodes, ophthalmoplegia, myopathy or lactate data; no maternal family history.
Infectious or inflammatory calcification
For: low socioeconomic setting; febrile onset; raised inflammatory markers.
Against: the calcification is symmetric and confined to deep structures rather than scattered; no cystic lesions are described.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT: Wilson disease and the parathyroid disorders are excluded by laboratory testing. A mitochondrial disorder is not supported by the phenotype. Infectious calcification does not fit the symmetric deep distribution. What remains is idiopathic bilateral striatopallidodentate calcification with its typical neuropsychiatric picture, so I commit to Fahr disease.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Fahr disease (primary/idiopathic bilateral basal ganglia calcification)

CASE: M082

KEY FINDINGS:
Painful lower-limb ulcers with well-demarcated, raised violaceous borders. Skin biopsy is consistent with pyoderma gangrenosum (PG), and the ulcers appeared during a prednisone taper, which suggests the disease responds to steroids.
Midline destructive disease: septal perforation and destruction of the hard palate and orbital floor, in a person who uses intranasal cocaine.
Cavitary lung lesions that did not respond to antibiotics; cultures showed sterile neutrophilic infiltrates.
ANCA negative, autoimmune work-up negative, and biopsies lacked the classic features of granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA).
Normal serum protein electrophoresis and normal eosinophils, which argues against a paraproteinemia or eosinophilic granulomatosis.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Pyoderma gangrenosum with extracutaneous (pulmonary, sinonasal and orbital) involvement, in the setting of cocaine-induced midline destructive lesions (CIMDL)
Granulomatosis with polyangiitis, ANCA-negative
Cocaine/levamisole-induced vasculopathy
Chronic infection (fungal or mycobacterial)
Extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, nasal type
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
PG with extracutaneous involvement plus CIMDL
For: biopsy-proven PG; sterile neutrophilic lung lesions; flare when steroids were tapered; septal and palatal destruction explained by cocaine.
Against: lung and orbital PG is rare.
ANCA-negative GPA
For: the triad of sinus, lung and skin disease; cavitating nodules; orbital involvement.
Against: ANCA negative; no granulomatous vasculitis on biopsy; cocaine alone can explain the midline destruction.
Levamisole/cocaine vasculopathy
For: active cocaine use.
Against: no retiform purpura or ear-lobe necrosis; ANCA negative (levamisole usually produces high-titre ANCA); histology shows PG rather than vasculitis.
Infection
For: cavitation; sinus disease.
Against: cultures negative; no response to antibiotics; the lesions respond to steroids.
NK/T-cell lymphoma
For: midline destruction.
Against: no atypical lymphoid infiltrate on biopsy.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT: Infection is excluded by negative cultures and antibiotic failure. Lymphoma is excluded by histology. GPA and levamisole vasculopathy are excluded by negative serology and the absence of vasculitic or granulomatous histology. A single sterile neutrophilic process that responds to steroids and involves skin, lung and orbit, with cocaine accounting for the midline destruction, best explains everything. I commit to PG with systemic involvement.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Pyoderma gangrenosum with extracutaneous (pulmonary and sinonasal/orbital) involvement, associated with cocaine-induced midline destructive lesions; GPA mimic

CASE: M083

KEY FINDINGS:
Acute viral prodrome (fever, cough, coryza), then wheeze, tachypnea (RR 62), subcostal retractions and tachycardia. This is lower respiratory tract obstruction, i.e., bronchiolitis or a wheezing illness.
Bilateral hyperinflation on chest X-ray, with no consolidation, which is consistent with small-airway disease.
Mildly raised CRP and a sterile blood culture, which favour a viral rather than bacterial cause.
Nasopharyngeal RT-PCR positive for a non-RSV virus (Ct 28, a substantial viral load); RSV negative.
Microcytic hypochromic anemia, which is incidental (likely iron deficiency).
DIFFERENTIAL:
Human metapneumovirus (hMPV) bronchiolitis
RSV bronchiolitis
Rhinovirus or other viral wheeze
Bacterial pneumonia
Foreign-body aspiration or early asthma
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
hMPV bronchiolitis
For: a picture clinically identical to RSV but with RSV PCR negative; positive targeted RT-PCR; hyperinflation; toddler age.
Against: the virus name is redacted, so its identity is inferred rather than confirmed.
RSV
For: classic presentation.
Against: PCR negative.
Rhinovirus or other virus
For: common cause of wheeze.
Against: the case emphasizes a specific positive PCR as the explanation.
Bacterial pneumonia
Against: no consolidation, sterile blood culture, low CRP.
Foreign body or asthma
Against: febrile coryzal onset; bilateral symmetric findings; no history of choking.

FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Human metapneumovirus (hMPV) acute bronchiolitis

CASE: M084

KEY FINDINGS:
Absent posterior septum pellucidum. The crura of the fornix are inferiorly displaced and not joined by a commissure. This is a midline developmental anomaly.
Lateral ventricles mildly prominent; third and fourth ventricles normal. This argues against hydrocephalus-induced destruction of the septum.
No other anomalies: the corpus callosum, cortex and optic structures are not reported abnormal, and there is no visual or endocrine complaint.
20-year history of generalized tonic-clonic seizures with a normal neurological examination.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Isolated partial (posterior) agenesis of the septum pellucidum
Septo-optic dysplasia
Lobar holoprosencephaly or the septopreoptic variant
Septal absence secondary to schizencephaly, callosal agenesis or polymicrogyria
Acquired septal fenestration from chronic hydrocephalus
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Isolated partial septal agenesis
For: imaging confined to the septum and fornix; no other malformation; normal examination.
Against: the association with epilepsy is debated, but it is reported.
Septo-optic dysplasia
For: absent septum.
Against: no visual deficit, optic nerve hypoplasia or pituitary dysfunction; the absence is partial and posterior, whereas SOD typically involves the anterior septum.
Holoprosencephaly
Against: the hemispheres are separated; the frontal horns and third ventricle are normal.
Schizencephaly, callosal agenesis or polymicrogyria
Against: the MRI shows no other anomaly.
Hydrocephalus-related fenestration
Against: ventricles only mildly prominent; third and fourth ventricles normal.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT: SOD is excluded clinically and by the location of the defect. Holoprosencephaly and the associated cortical or callosal malformations are excluded on imaging. Hydrocephalus is excluded by the normal ventricular system. I commit to isolated posterior septal agenesis with fornix maldevelopment, presenting as epilepsy.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Isolated partial (posterior) agenesis of the septum pellucidum with fornix anomaly, presenting with epilepsy

CASE: M085

Sarcomatoid HCC or sarcomatoid cholangiocarcinoma

Metastatic leiomyosarcoma

Dedifferentiated leiomyosarcoma

Sarcomatoid HCC or cholangiocarcinoma
For: HBV background.

GIST
For: hepatogastric location; spindle cells.
Against: desmin and caldesmon positivity is atypical; final pathology favours smooth-muscle tumour.
PEComa
For: SMA positive; spindle cells.

Metastasis
Against: no other primary is described.

CASE: M086

Ovarian carcinosarcoma (malignant mixed Müllerian tumour)
Primary ovarian leiomyosarcoma (not teratoma-derived)
Immature teratoma
Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour or other sarcoma arising in a teratoma

Leiomyosarcoma in teratoma

Carcinosarcoma
For: age; aggressive behaviour.
Against: no carcinomatous component; normal CA-125.

CASE: M087

Slow-growing, asymptomatic, lobulated lesion that looks submucosal, near the GE junction, over three years, after treated H. pylori gastritis.

Gastritis cystica profunda / submucosal heterotopic glands
GIST or leiomyoma
Well-differentiated adenocarcinoma with submucosal invasion

Inverted polyp

Inflammatory fibroid polyp

CASE: M088

KEY FINDINGS:
Focal seizures with visual aura (blurred vision), ictal nystagmus and preserved awareness. This points to an occipital (posterior cortex) seizure onset.
Epileptiform activity in the right occipital region spreading to the right temporal and parietal regions, which matches the lesion side. The right-sided clonic movements suggest spread or a possible laterality inconsistency in the report.
Cortical lesion in the right parieto-occipital region with a bulging (expanded) gyrus, T1 isointense and only slightly T2/FLAIR hyperintense, with perifocal edema.
Epilepsy persisted while the EEG fluctuated, which fits a structural, lesional epilepsy.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Low-grade diffuse glioma (pediatric-type, e.g., diffuse astrocytoma or angiocentric glioma)
Dysembryoplastic neuroepithelial tumour (DNET)
Ganglioglioma
Focal cortical dysplasia
Peri-ictal MRI changes or focal encephalitis (e.g., MOG-associated cortical encephalitis)
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Diffuse low-grade glioma
For: gyral expansion; T1 isointense with only slight T2 hyperintensity; epilepsy in a child.
Against: perifocal edema is uncommon in low-grade tumours; no histology.
DNET
For: cortical location; child; drug-resistant focal epilepsy.
Against: DNETs are usually markedly T2 hyperintense, "bubbly" and T1 hypointense, without edema.
Ganglioglioma
For: the most common epilepsy-associated tumour in children.
Against: no cyst, calcification or enhancement described.
Focal cortical dysplasia
For: epilepsy; cortical signal change.
Against: mass effect and edema are atypical; no transmantle sign.
Peri-ictal change or encephalitis
For: edema; fluctuating EEG.
Against: no infection or inflammatory signs; the mass-like gyral expansion persisted.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT: FCD and peri-ictal or inflammatory causes do not explain a persistent mass-like gyral expansion. The near-isointense signal argues against a typical DNET or ganglioglioma. I commit to a low-grade diffuse glioma, with the caveat that histology is needed to confirm it.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Low-grade diffuse glioma of the right parieto-occipital cortex (pediatric-type) presenting with focal occipital-lobe epilepsy
