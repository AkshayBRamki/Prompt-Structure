CASE: M089

KEY FINDINGS:
A live intrauterine pregnancy (7 wk, FHR 132) and, at the same time, a live right adnexal pregnancy (6w4d, FHR 112). Two separate gestations in two separate sites is the decisive finding.
Pathology confirmed the ectopic pregnancy.
β-hCG of 142,946 mIU/mL is very high. That fits two gestations.
Sharp lower abdominal pain with dark bleeding fits ectopic implantation and a subchorionic hemorrhage.
A 7 cm right paratubal cyst is an incidental, pathology-confirmed finding that could add to the pain.
Vitals were stable, so there is no evidence of rupture.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Heterotopic pregnancy
Isolated tubal ectopic pregnancy
Threatened miscarriage with subchorionic hemorrhage (intrauterine only)
Twin intrauterine pregnancy
Adnexal torsion due to the paratubal cyst
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Heterotopic pregnancy
For: both an intrauterine and an adnexal live embryo are seen, the ectopic is confirmed on pathology, and hCG is very high.
Against: none.
Isolated ectopic pregnancy
For: adnexal gestation, pain, and bleeding.
Against: there is also a live intrauterine pregnancy.
Threatened miscarriage / subchorionic hemorrhage
For: bleeding and a subchorionic hemorrhage on imaging.
Against: it does not explain the live adnexal embryo.
Twin intrauterine pregnancy
For: two embryos and high hCG.
Against: one embryo is outside the uterus.
Adnexal torsion
For: 7 cm cyst and sharp pain.
Against: no torsion is described, and the pathology shows a cyst only.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Isolated ectopic, twin IUP, and threatened miscarriage alone are excluded, because the imaging shows simultaneous intrauterine and extrauterine gestations.
Torsion is not supported.
I commit to heterotopic pregnancy, with a subchorionic hemorrhage and a paratubal cyst present as well.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Heterotopic pregnancy (live intrauterine pregnancy plus right adnexal/tubal ectopic pregnancy), with subchorionic hemorrhage and a right paratubal cyst

CASE: M090

KEY FINDINGS:
Motor regression at 20 months (loss of high kneeling). This points to a progressive neuromuscular disease, not just the hypotonia of Down syndrome.
Symmetric hypotonia, reduced reflexes, weakness, and hand tremor (fasciculation-like). This is a lower motor neuron pattern.
SMN1 exon 7–8 homozygous deletion, which is diagnostic of SMA.
SMN2 copy gain, which fits a milder SMA type.
Mosaic trisomy 21 is confirmed on karyotype.
A sibling has SMA type 2, consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Spinal muscular atrophy type 2, coexisting with mosaic Down syndrome
Hypotonia due to Down syndrome alone
Duchenne/Becker muscular dystrophy
Congenital myopathy
Hereditary neuropathy
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
SMA type 2
For: regression, areflexia/hyporeflexia, tremor, the genetic result, and an affected sibling.
Against: none.
Down syndrome hypotonia alone
For: the trisomy 21 mosaicism.
Against: Down syndrome does not cause motor regression or loss of reflexes, and SMN1 is deleted.
DMD/BMD
For: a boy with motor delay.
Against: reflexes and the genetic findings point to SMA, and dystrophin was not implicated.
Congenital myopathy
For: hypotonia.
Against: it is usually non-progressive, and SMA was confirmed.
Neuropathy
For: hyporeflexia.
Against: SMN1 deletion explains the whole picture.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT: The alternatives are excluded by the motor regression and the molecular confirmation. I commit to SMA type 2 as a second, coexisting diagnosis alongside mosaic trisomy 21.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Spinal muscular atrophy type 2 (homozygous SMN1 exon 7–8 deletion) in a child with mosaic trisomy 21

CASE: M091

KEY FINDINGS:
Multiple liver masses on imaging, with pancreatic infiltration. This indicates metastatic disease.
A hypervascular nodule attached to the bladder, and cystoscopy/biopsy confirmed the bladder as the primary site.
Liver biopsy showed poorly differentiated carcinoma.
The immunoprofile (CK20+, focal CK7+) fits a urothelial/bladder origin.
Several stains argue against other primaries:
TTF-1 negative argues against lung.
PAX8 negative argues against kidney and Müllerian origin.
HepPar-1 negative argues against hepatocellular carcinoma.
GATA3 is negative. This is seen in a subset of poorly differentiated urothelial carcinomas.
Normal tumor markers argue against GI, pancreatic, and ovarian primaries.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Metastatic primary bladder carcinoma (poorly differentiated urothelial carcinoma)
Primary bladder adenocarcinoma (enteric type)
Metastatic colorectal adenocarcinoma involving the bladder
Pancreatic adenocarcinoma
Lymphoma
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Bladder urothelial carcinoma
For: confirmed on cystoscopy, the bladder mass, smoking history, and CK7/CK20 co-expression.
Against: GATA3 is negative and CK7 is only focal.
Bladder adenocarcinoma
For: CK20+ with GATA3 negative.
Against: no glandular differentiation was described.
Colorectal
For: CK20+.
Against: CEA normal, no colonic lesion, and the bladder was confirmed as primary.
Pancreatic
For: pancreatic infiltration.
Against: CA19-9 normal, and the bladder was proven primary.
Lymphoma
For: son with lymphoma.
Against: the tumor is a cytokeratin-positive carcinoma.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Lymphoma is excluded because the tumor is keratin-positive.
HCC, lung, and kidney primaries are excluded by IHC.
GI and pancreatic primaries are excluded by the cystoscopic proof of a bladder primary and normal markers.
I commit to a poorly differentiated bladder carcinoma (urothelial) with liver and pancreatic metastases. Enteric-type adenocarcinoma remains a histologic subtype consideration.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Metastatic poorly differentiated primary bladder (urothelial) carcinoma with liver and pancreatic metastases

CASE: M092

KEY FINDINGS:
Long-standing chronic calcific pancreatitis, a known risk factor for pancreatic cancer. Its risk is raised further here by smoking and alcohol history.

Cystic lesions in the body/tail.

GI bleeding.
Biopsy confirmed malignancy.

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma arising in chronic pancreatitis
Mass-forming (focal) chronic pancreatitis
Intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm (IPMN) with associated invasive carcinoma
Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor
Pancreatic pseudocyst/pseudoaneurysm causing bleeding

PDAC
For: very high CA19-9, a hypoechoic mass with nodes, risk factors, and biopsy proof.
Against: none.
Mass-forming pancreatitis
For: chronic pancreatitis background.
Against: the degree of CA19-9 elevation, malignant nodes, and the biopsy.
IPMN-associated carcinoma
For: cystic lesions.
Against: the dominant finding is a solid hypoechoic mass.
pNET
For: pancreatic mass.
Against: CA19-9 elevation and the hypoechoic, infiltrative pattern.

For: bleeding and cysts.

CASE: M093

KEY FINDINGS:
A polypoid growth on the floor of the mouth, slow-growing over three years.
Histology: polygonal and spindle cells with granular cytoplasm, arranged in sheets and fascicles.
Malignant features: pleomorphism, prominent nucleoli, necrosis, atypical mitoses, and apoptotic bodies.
Immunoprofile:
Vimentin, NSE, and CD10 positive.
S100 negative, which excludes a conventional (Schwann cell) granular cell tumor and melanoma.
Pan-CK, CK5/6, and p40 negative, which excludes squamous and other carcinomas.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Non-neural (primitive polypoid) granular cell tumor with malignant/atypical features
Undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma
Conventional (S100+) malignant granular cell tumor
Spindle cell (sarcomatoid) squamous carcinoma
Amelanotic melanoma
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Non-neural granular cell tumor
For: polypoid gross shape, granular cytoplasm in polygonal/spindle cells, S100 negative, NSE+, CD10+, vimentin+, and slow growth.
Against: marked pleomorphism and necrosis are more than is typical, which argues for an atypical or malignant form.
Undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma
For: pleomorphism, vimentin+, CD10+, and necrosis.
Against: granular cytoplasm, NSE positivity, a low mitotic count, and a three-year indolent course.
Conventional granular cell tumor
For: granular cells.
Against: S100 negative.
Sarcomatoid SCC
For: oral site and spindle cells.
Against: p40, CK5/6, and Pan-CK all negative.
Melanoma
For: pleomorphic epithelioid/spindle cells.
Against: S100 negative.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Carcinoma, melanoma, and S100-positive granular cell tumor are excluded by IHC.
UPS is a diagnosis of exclusion and is less fitting given the granular cytoplasm, NSE positivity, and indolent polypoid course.
I commit to a malignant (atypical) non-neural granular cell tumor.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Malignant (atypical) non-neural granular cell tumor of the floor of the mouth

CASE: M094

KEY FINDINGS:
Prior blunt left chest trauma with rib fractures and a chest tube, two years earlier. This sets up an occult diaphragmatic injury.
Bowel sounds heard in the left chest, with absent breath sounds and tachypnea.

Normal blood tests and a soft abdomen argue against strangulation at this stage.

Delayed post-traumatic left diaphragmatic hernia with bowel obstruction
Congenital (Bochdalek) diaphragmatic hernia
Diaphragmatic eventration
Adhesive small bowel obstruction
Left pleural effusion/hemothorax
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Traumatic diaphragmatic hernia
For: trauma history, a focal defect, herniated bowel, and obstruction.
Against: none.
Bochdalek hernia
For: left-sided location.
Against: adult presentation after a clear traumatic injury.
Eventration
For: elevated abdominal contents.
Against: CT shows a true defect rather than an intact, thinned diaphragm.
Adhesive SBO
For: obstruction.
Against: the bowel is in the thorax, and there was no abdominal surgery.
Effusion/hemothorax
For: absent breath sounds.
Against: bowel loops are seen on imaging.

FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Delayed post-traumatic left diaphragmatic hernia with herniated small bowel causing intestinal obstruction

CASE: M095

KEY FINDINGS:
Three years of abemaciclib, a CDK4/6 inhibitor with known pneumonitis risk.
New cough and a fall in oxygen saturation.
HRCT shows subpleural ground-glass opacities and consolidation in an organizing pneumonia pattern.
Pulmonary function tests are restrictive with reduced diffusion capacity.
BAL shows lymphocytosis of 89%, which supports drug-induced or hypersensitivity lung disease.
Histology shows fibrosing organizing pneumonia.
Infection, malignancy, and autoimmune disease were ruled out, and she had no prior ILD.
The DLST was negative. This test has low sensitivity, so a negative result does not exclude the drug.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Abemaciclib-induced interstitial lung disease (fibrosing organizing pneumonia)
Cryptogenic organizing pneumonia
Infectious pneumonia
Lymphangitic carcinomatosis or pulmonary metastasis of breast cancer
Connective tissue disease–associated ILD
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Abemaciclib ILD
For: chronic exposure to the drug, the OP pattern, BAL lymphocytosis, and exclusion of other causes.
Against: negative DLST, which has low sensitivity.
Cryptogenic OP
For: OP histology.
Against: there is an identifiable drug cause, so the OP is not cryptogenic.
Infection
For: cough and malaise.
Against: BAL and serology were negative.
Metastasis
For: recurrent breast cancer.
Against: BAL and biopsy show no malignancy.
CTD-ILD
For: OP pattern.
Against: autoantibodies negative.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT: Infection, malignancy, and autoimmune disease are excluded by tests. Cryptogenic OP is excluded because there is a plausible drug cause. I commit to abemaciclib-induced ILD.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Abemaciclib-induced interstitial lung disease presenting as fibrosing organizing pneumonia

CASE: M096

KEY FINDINGS:
Wound breakdown after surgery with fever, leukocytosis, progressive necrosis, and induration.
All cultures negative, and no response to antibiotics.
Worsening after debridement (pathergy).
A daughter had a similar process after breast reduction, which suggests a predisposition.
Pembrolizumab exposure, an immune dysregulation risk factor.
Skin biopsy consistent with the diagnosis.
Together this is the classic picture of postsurgical pyoderma gangrenosum.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Postsurgical pyoderma gangrenosum
Necrotizing soft tissue infection / surgical site infection
Ischemic mastectomy skin-flap necrosis
Pembrolizumab-induced immune-related cutaneous toxicity
Sweet syndrome
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Postsurgical PG
For: sterile cultures, antibiotic failure, worsening after debridement, the family history, and the biopsy.
Against: none.
Necrotizing infection
For: fever, leukocytosis, and necrosis.
Against: all cultures negative and no antibiotic response.
Flap ischemia
For: skin necrosis after skin-sparing mastectomy.
Against: fever, leukocytosis, and progression after debridement.
irAE
For: pembrolizumab exposure.
Against: the lesions are localized to surgical sites, the biopsy points to PG, and there is a family pattern.
Sweet syndrome
For: fever and neutrophilic process.
Against: ulcerative, necrotic wound breakdown rather than plaques.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT: Infection is excluded by cultures and treatment failure. Ischemia and Sweet syndrome do not fit the course. Pembrolizumab may have contributed as a trigger. I commit to PG.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Postsurgical pyoderma gangrenosum after bilateral mastectomy
