CASE: M105

KEY FINDINGS:
Adult-onset, slowly progressive focal right lower limb dystonia, starting in the foot and spreading over 3 years. It improves with sensory tricks and backward walking, which shows it is a true dystonia, but adult leg-onset spreading dystonia suggests a secondary or neurodegenerative cause.

Parents were first cousins. One sibling had seizures and psychiatric illness; another died at age 5 of an unknown illness. This points to autosomal recessive inheritance with variable severity within the family.
Homozygous missense variant in the patient and a heterozygous variant in an unaffected sibling. This fits recessive segregation.
Skin biopsy electron microscopy shows granular osmiophilic deposits (GRODs). This is the ultrastructural hallmark of PPT1-related neuronal ceroid lipofuscinosis (CLN1).
Normal cognition (MoCA 30/30), normal copper and ceruloplasmin, and negative autoimmune and paraneoplastic panels.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Adult-onset neuronal ceroid lipofuscinosis, CLN1 type (PPT1-related)
Other GROD-forming NCL (CLN10/CTSD; CLN4/DNAJC5)
Wilson disease
Dopa-responsive dystonia
Autosomal recessive spinocerebellar ataxia with dystonia (e.g., SCAR/ARCA forms, ataxia-telangiectasia variant)
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
CLN1 NCL
For: GRODs on skin electron microscopy, consanguinity, homozygous variant segregating with disease, cerebellar atrophy and ataxia, and a severely affected sibling with seizures, psychiatric illness and childhood death (the classic infantile/juvenile spectrum).
Against: no seizures, vision loss or dementia in the patient; dystonia as the presenting feature is atypical; the variant is classed as of uncertain significance.
CLN10 or CLN4
For: these can also show GRODs.
Against: CLN4 is usually autosomal dominant and does not fit a consanguineous recessive pattern; CLN10 is rarer and usually more severe; the genetic result points to a single recessive gene.
Wilson disease
For: young adult with dystonia.
Against: normal copper and ceruloplasmin, normal liver tests, cerebellar atrophy without basal ganglia lesions, and the GRODs.
Dopa-responsive dystonia
For: leg-onset dystonia and transient levodopa benefit.
Against: adult onset, no sustained levodopa response, ataxia, cerebellar atrophy and GRODs.
Recessive SCA or ARCA with dystonia
For: ataxia plus dystonia in a consanguineous family.
Against: the storage material on electron microscopy is specific to NCL.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT: I exclude Wilson disease on the normal copper studies and dopa-responsive dystonia on the lack of a sustained levodopa response and the presence of cerebellar involvement. I exclude a non-NCL recessive ataxia because GRODs indicate a lysosomal ceroid storage disease. Among GROD-forming NCLs, the recessive inheritance, homozygosity and family phenotype favor PPT1 (CLN1). I commit to adult-onset CLN1, presenting atypically as focal limb dystonia with cerebellar ataxia.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Adult-onset neuronal ceroid lipofuscinosis type 1 (CLN1, homozygous PPT1-related) presenting as progressive focal lower limb dystonia with cerebellar ataxia
