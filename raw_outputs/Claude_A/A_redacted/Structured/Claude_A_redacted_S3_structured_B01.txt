CASE: M001

KEY FINDINGS:
Multiple bead-like intradural nodules from L1 to S1, homogeneously enhancing, with no cyst, hemorrhage or necrosis. This pattern suggests multiple benign nerve sheath tumors.
A prior perineal schwannoma was resected, a mobile perineal nodule has now recurred, and the canal lesions are pathology-confirmed schwannoma. That makes at least two non-intradermal schwannomas in separate locations.
Head, cervical and thoracic imaging is normal, so there are no vestibular schwannomas or meningiomas, which argues against NF2.
Germline testing is negative for NF2, SMARCB1 and LZTR1, and there is no family history.
There is no neurological deficit. Chronic constipation may be a mild cauda equina effect.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Schwannomatosis (genetically unexplained, "not elsewhere classified")
NF2-related schwannomatosis (mosaic)
Neurofibromatosis type 1 with multiple spinal neurofibromas
Myxopapillary ependymoma with CSF seeding
Leptomeningeal metastases or drop metastases
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Schwannomatosis.
For: multiple pathology-proven schwannomas in separate sites, spinal and peripheral predominance, no vestibular tumors, no neurological deficit.
Against: no pathogenic variant was found, but that is expected in this category.
Mosaic NF2.
For: blood testing can miss mosaic NF2; multiple schwannomas.
Against: normal head imaging with no vestibular schwannomas, meningiomas or ependymomas.
NF1 neurofibromas.
For: multiple spinal nerve sheath tumors.
Against: histology is schwannoma, and there are no café-au-lait macules or other NF1 stigmata.
Myxopapillary ependymoma with seeding.
For: lumbosacral intradural location.
Against: histology is schwannoma, and the rest of the neuraxis is normal.
Metastases.
For: multiple intradural nodules.
Against: no primary cancer, years of stability (2022 to 2024), benign pathology.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
NF1, ependymoma and metastases are excluded by histology.
NF2 is unlikely because there are no cranial or vestibular lesions and germline NF2 testing is negative. Tumor-tissue NF2 analysis could still exclude mosaicism formally.
I commit to schwannomatosis because she has at least two pathologically confirmed non-intradermal schwannomas without vestibular schwannoma.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Schwannomatosis (no NF2, SMARCB1 or LZTR1 variant identified, i.e. schwannomatosis-NEC), with multiple lumbosacral intraspinal schwannomas and recurrent perineal schwannoma.

CASE: M002

KEY FINDINGS:
Synchronous bilateral breast carcinomas. Both are poorly differentiated (G3), node-negative, triple-negative, with Ki-67 of 50%.
After surgery there are new lung, adrenal and subcutaneous lesions.
The lung biopsy is morphologically similar to the breast tumors.
The lung tumor is CK7-positive and negative for TTF-1, napsin A and p40, which argues against primary lung adenocarcinoma and squamous carcinoma.
GATA3 is negative. This is a known pitfall, because GATA3 sensitivity is reduced in triple-negative breast cancer.
The lung tumor has no actionable lung driver alterations.
There is a strong family history of cancer, including breast cancer in a paternal aunt.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Metastatic triple-negative breast carcinoma (lung, adrenal, subcutaneous)
Primary TTF-1-negative lung adenocarcinoma, synchronous with the breast cancers
Metastatic carcinoma of another primary, such as upper GI or pancreaticobiliary
Primary lung squamous or large-cell carcinoma
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Metastatic breast carcinoma.
For: known aggressive bilateral triple-negative disease, the same morphology in the lung, CK7 positivity, a lung marker profile that is negative.
Against: GATA3 negativity and node-negative primaries. Neither is unusual in triple-negative breast cancer.
Primary lung adenocarcinoma.
For: a spiculated lung nodule and a CK7-positive carcinoma.
Against: TTF-1 and napsin A are negative, there are no driver mutations, and the morphology mirrors the breast tumors.
Another primary.
For: CK7-positive carcinoma of unknown origin.
Against: no GI or pancreatic lesion is described, and the morphology resembles the breast tumors.
Lung squamous or large-cell carcinoma.
Against: p40 is negative and the morphology is not consistent.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Primary lung carcinoma is excluded by the immunoprofile (TTF-1, napsin A and p40 negative) and the absence of driver mutations.
Another primary is unsupported.
I commit to metastatic breast origin, based on matching morphology and the known high-grade triple-negative primaries. Germline testing (BRCA1/2 and similar) is warranted given the family history.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Metastatic triple-negative breast carcinoma (from synchronous bilateral invasive ductal carcinoma) to the lung, adrenal gland and subcutaneous tissue.

CASE: M003

KEY FINDINGS:
A 2-year-old boy with slowly progressive, painless, massive abdominal distension and left scrotal swelling.
The scrotal swelling transilluminates and is partially reducible, so it is fluid-filled and communicates through the inguinal canal.
CT shows a 25 × 30 cm multiloculated cyst with internal septations, extending from the abdomen through the inguinal canal into the scrotum and displacing the bowel.
The left testis is not palpable, likely displaced or compressed.
Labs are normal and there is no solid component.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Abdominoscrotal cystic lymphangioma (lymphatic malformation)
Abdominoscrotal hydrocele
Mesenteric or omental cyst with inguinal extension
Cystic teratoma
Hydrocele with indirect inguinal hernia
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Lymphangioma.
For: multiloculated with septations, very large, insidious growth in early childhood, transilluminates, extends across tissue planes.
Against: nothing substantial.
Abdominoscrotal hydrocele.
For: transilluminant scrotal swelling with an abdominal component that communicates through the canal.
Against: it is typically unilocular ("hourglass"), and this lesion is multiloculated and septated.
Mesenteric or omental cyst.
For: a multiloculated abdominal cyst.
Against: scrotal extension through the canal is atypical.
Cystic teratoma.
For: a pediatric cystic mass.
Against: no fat, calcification or solid elements.
Hernia with hydrocele.
For: partially reducible.
Against: no bowel in the sac, and the CT shows a cystic mass.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Teratoma and hernia are excluded by the CT.
Simple hydrocele and mesenteric cyst fit less well because of the multiloculation and the canal extension.
I commit to a lymphatic malformation, consistent with the histopathology.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Giant abdominoscrotal cystic lymphangioma (macrocystic lymphatic malformation).

CASE: M004

KEY FINDINGS:
A 2.4 cm well-circumscribed breast mass with a mucinous cut surface.
The tumor is a high-grade adenocarcinoma with mucin secretion and papillary architecture.
It is CK7-positive and negative for CK20, CDX-2, SATB2 and PAX-8, which argues against GI and Müllerian origin.
Breast markers GCDFP-15 and mammaglobin are weakly positive.
It is triple-negative with Ki-67 of 70% and strong p53 expression.
CEA is elevated, and PET shows no other primary.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Primary mucinous cystadenocarcinoma of the breast
Pure mucinous (colloid) carcinoma of the breast
Metastatic mucinous adenocarcinoma from ovary, GI tract, pancreas or lung
Invasive papillary or micropapillary carcinoma with mucin production
Triple-negative invasive carcinoma of no special type with mucinous differentiation
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Mucinous cystadenocarcinoma.
For: intracellular mucin, papillary and cystic architecture, triple-negative, CK7-positive and CK20-negative, postmenopausal-age woman, circumscribed, node-negative.
Against: it is very rare.
Pure mucinous carcinoma.
For: mucinous, circumscribed tumor.
Against: it is usually ER/PR-positive and low-grade with extracellular mucin pools, whereas this is triple-negative and high-grade.
Metastasis.
For: mucinous histology and high CEA.
Against: the GI and Müllerian markers are negative, breast markers are positive, and PET shows no other lesion.
Papillary or micropapillary carcinoma.
For: papillary formation.
Against: mucin secretion dominates, and micropapillary carcinoma is usually ER-positive.
Triple-negative NST.
For: high grade and triple-negative.
Against: the distinctive mucinous and papillary features.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Metastasis is excluded by the immunoprofile and the PET.
Colloid and micropapillary carcinoma are unlikely given the receptor status and grade.
I commit to primary mucinous cystadenocarcinoma.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Primary mucinous cystadenocarcinoma of the left breast (triple-negative, pT2N0).

CASE: M005

KEY FINDINGS:
Acute odynophagia, retrosternal burning and mild hematemesis.
Endoscopy shows multiple small "volcano-like" ulcers with raised erythematous edges in the mid-esophagus, with normal surrounding mucosa. This pattern is classic for herpes simplex.
Biopsy shows multinucleation, nuclear molding and enlargement, and ground-glass nuclei. These are the "3 Ms" of HSV.
Serology was initially negative, with IgG seroconversion at 2 months, indicating primary infection.
She is immunocompetent: HIV is negative and glucose is normal.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Herpes simplex virus esophagitis (primary infection)
Cytomegalovirus esophagitis
Pill-induced esophagitis
Reflux esophagitis
Candida esophagitis
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
HSV.
For: volcano ulcers, ground-glass multinucleated cells, seroconversion.
Against: she is immunocompetent, but HSV esophagitis is well described in healthy adults.
CMV.
For: viral ulcers.
Against: CMV usually causes large, deep linear ulcers with owl's-eye inclusions in endothelial and stromal cells, and it is rare in immunocompetent patients.
Pill-induced esophagitis.
For: mid-esophageal ulcers.
Against: no culprit medication is described, and the histology shows viral changes.
Reflux esophagitis.
For: prior heartburn.
Against: reflux affects the distal esophagus, and the histology shows viral changes.
Candida.
Against: no plaques and no fungal elements.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
The histology and seroconversion exclude the non-viral causes and CMV.
I commit to primary HSV esophagitis in an immunocompetent host.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Herpes simplex virus esophagitis (primary infection) in an immunocompetent patient.

CASE: M006

KEY FINDINGS:
A submucosal gastric mass along the lesser curvature, initially suspected to be a GIST.
Histology shows a spindle-cell proliferation.
It is S100-positive and negative for CD117, DOG-1 and CD34, which excludes GIST. It is negative for SMA and desmin, which excludes smooth-muscle tumors.
There is no necrosis, lymphadenopathy or metastasis.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Gastric schwannoma
Gastrointestinal stromal tumor (GIST)
Leiomyoma or leiomyosarcoma
Solitary fibrous tumor or inflammatory fibroid polyp
Malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumor
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Schwannoma.
For: S100-positive spindle cells, negative for KIT, DOG-1, CD34 and the muscle markers, indolent course, young woman.
Against: nothing significant.
GIST.
For: the CT appearance and location.
Against: CD117 and DOG-1 are negative.
Smooth-muscle tumors.
Against: SMA and desmin are negative.
Solitary fibrous tumor or inflammatory fibroid polyp.
Against: CD34 is negative.
MPNST.
For: S100-positive.
Against: no necrosis, invasion or metastasis, and MPNST typically shows only patchy S100.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
The immunohistochemistry excludes GIST, smooth-muscle tumors, solitary fibrous tumor and inflammatory fibroid polyp.
The benign behavior argues against MPNST.
I commit to gastric schwannoma.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Gastric schwannoma.

CASE: M007

KEY FINDINGS:
An asymptomatic primigravida at 12 weeks.
The gestational sac lies near the left uterine horn with an empty endometrial cavity.
MRI confirms a live 12-week fetus (CRL 5.8 cm) within the left fallopian tube.
There are no peritoneal signs, which indicates the pregnancy is unruptured.
Survival to 12 weeks near the cornu suggests implantation in the interstitial portion of the tube, which is surrounded by myometrium.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Unruptured left tubal ectopic pregnancy, interstitial portion
Ampullary or isthmic tubal ectopic pregnancy
Angular (eccentric intrauterine) pregnancy
Rudimentary horn pregnancy
Abdominal pregnancy
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Interstitial tubal pregnancy.
For: located near the horn, empty cavity, MRI shows the sac in the tube, unruptured late survival.
Against: nothing significant.
Ampullary or isthmic pregnancy.
For: located in the tube.
Against: these usually rupture by 6 to 8 weeks, and this sac is next to the horn.
Angular pregnancy.
For: located near the horn.
Against: the endometrial cavity is empty and MRI places the sac in the tube.
Rudimentary horn pregnancy.
For: located near the horn and survived late.
Against: no Müllerian anomaly was identified.
Abdominal pregnancy.
Against: MRI shows the sac in the tube.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
MRI excludes intrauterine, abdominal and horn pregnancies.
The cornual location and survival to 12 weeks favor the interstitial segment.
I commit to an unruptured tubal ectopic pregnancy.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Unruptured live left tubal (interstitial) ectopic pregnancy at 12 weeks' gestation.

CASE: M008

KEY FINDINGS:
Known metastatic small bowel neuroendocrine tumor with a mesenteric mass encasing the SMA and SMV.
Several months of postprandial pain and reduced food intake, which suggests chronic mesenteric ischemia ("intestinal angina").
Now sudden pain rated 10/10, out of proportion to the exam.
Leukocytosis.
CT shows reduced bowel-wall enhancement and wall thickening in right lower quadrant small bowel loops.
Lactate is normal, which does not exclude early ischemia.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Acute-on-chronic mesenteric ischemia from tumor or desmoplastic encasement of the SMA and SMV
Small bowel obstruction or volvulus from mesenteric fibrosis
SMV thrombosis (venous ischemia)
Carcinoid crisis
Perforation
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Mesenteric ischemia.
For: chronic postprandial pain followed by acute severe pain, bowel hypoenhancement, vessel encasement, leukocytosis.
Against: normal lactate, which is an insensitive marker early on.
Small bowel obstruction.
For: nausea and vomiting, mesenteric fibrosis.
Against: no dilated loops or transition point are reported.
SMV thrombosis.
For: SMV involvement and wall thickening.
Against: no thrombus is described. This may still be part of the ischemic mechanism.
Carcinoid crisis.
Against: no flushing, hemodynamic instability or bronchospasm.
Perforation.
Against: no free air or peritonitis is described.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Carcinoid crisis and perforation are excluded clinically and radiologically.
Obstruction is not supported by the imaging.
I commit to acute-on-chronic mesenteric ischemia caused by tumor and desmoplastic encasement of the mesenteric vessels.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Acute-on-chronic mesenteric ischemia of the small bowel secondary to metastatic SBNET mesenteric mass encasing the SMA and SMV.
