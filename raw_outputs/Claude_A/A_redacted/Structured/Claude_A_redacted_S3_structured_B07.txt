CASE: M049

KEY FINDINGS:
A synovial haemangioma of the right ankle was resected 11 years earlier. This makes a second vascular synovial lesion in the same limb plausible.
The knee has recurrent atraumatic pain and swelling, which suggests repeated haemarthroses.
X-ray shows epiphyseal overgrowth, a widened intercondylar notch, osteopenia and early degeneration in a 17-year-old. This "haemophilic-like" arthropathy is caused by chronic intra-articular bleeding and hyperaemia during growth.
MRI shows lobulated lesions infiltrating the suprapatellar and Hoffa's fat pads. They are T2/PD-hyperintense, enhance avidly, and are fed by popliteal arterial branches, which means a vascular tumour.
Histology confirms spindle cell haemangioma.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Intra-articular (synovial) spindle cell haemangioma with secondary haemophilic-like arthropathy
Tenosynovial giant cell tumour / pigmented villonodular synovitis (PVNS)
Haemophilic arthropathy
Synovial sarcoma
Lipoma arborescens
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Synovial haemangioma
For: prior haemangioma in the same limb, avid enhancement, arterial feeders, fat-pad infiltration, and the arthropathy pattern.
Against: none significant; the histology is diagnostic.
PVNS
For: recurrent swelling, a lobulated synovial mass, and bone remodelling.
Against: no haemosiderin "blooming" or low T2 signal is described, the lesion is T2-hyperintense with arterial feeders, and the histology differs.
Haemophilic arthropathy
For: identical radiographic features.
Against: no bleeding disorder or family history, and a mass lesion is present.
Synovial sarcoma
For: an enhancing periarticular mass in an adolescent.
Against: an 11-year vascular history, an infiltrative fat-pad pattern without aggressive destruction, and benign histology.
Lipoma arborescens
For: fat-pad and suprapatellar location.
Against: the signal is not fat-equivalent and enhancement is avid.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT: Histology excludes PVNS, sarcoma and lipoma arborescens. The absence of a coagulopathy history and the presence of a vascular mass exclude primary haemophilia. I commit to synovial spindle cell haemangioma causing recurrent haemarthrosis and secondary arthropathy.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Intra-articular (synovial) spindle cell haemangioma of the right knee with secondary haemophilic-like arthropathy and muscle atrophy

CASE: M050

KEY FINDINGS:
Sudden sleepiness with a normal brain CT argues against stroke or haemorrhage and points to a metabolic, respiratory or embolic cause.
Arterial gas shows hypoxaemia (PaO2 58) with mild hypercapnia and a raised bicarbonate (30), which fits a metabolic alkalosis with hypoventilation.
Atrial fibrillation, ejection fraction 25%, pulmonary artery systolic pressure 81 mmHg and bilateral effusions indicate severe heart failure with marked pulmonary hypertension.
A contrast chest CT was performed despite CKD. That implies a vascular question was specifically being investigated, most likely pulmonary embolism.
Other contributors to her drowsiness: anaemia (Hb 7.6), renal failure responding to dialysis, hyponatraemia, and a history of malignancy (a prothrombotic risk factor).
DIFFERENTIAL:
Acute pulmonary embolism on a background of HFrEF and pulmonary hypertension
Acute decompensated heart failure with hypercapnic/hypoxic encephalopathy
Uraemic/metabolic encephalopathy (AKI on CKD, hyponatraemia)
Asthma exacerbation with CO2 retention
Cardioembolic stroke from atrial fibrillation
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Pulmonary embolism
For: hypoxaemia, atrial fibrillation, prior cancer, immobility and age; very high PASP; and a contrast CT ordered despite renal risk.
Against: the result is redacted; the effusions and wheeze can be explained by heart failure.
Decompensated heart failure
For: EF 25%, bilateral effusions, and wheeze ("cardiac asthma").
Against: does not by itself explain a sudden change in consciousness.
Uraemic/metabolic encephalopathy
For: raised urea and creatinine that improved with dialysis, and hyponatraemia.
Against: the onset was sudden, and her CKD is chronic.
Asthma with CO2 retention
For: diffuse wheeze.
Against: PaCO2 is only 47, and pH 7.42 does not indicate significant CO2 narcosis.
Cardioembolic stroke
For: atrial fibrillation.
Against: normal brain CT and no focal deficits.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT: Stroke is unlikely given the normal CT and lack of focal signs. Asthma-related CO2 narcosis is unlikely given the near-normal pH. Uraemia and heart failure probably contribute but do not explain a sudden event or the targeted contrast CT. I commit to pulmonary embolism, with moderate confidence because the key CT result is redacted.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Acute pulmonary embolism in a patient with severe HFrEF, pulmonary hypertension and acute-on-chronic kidney disease

CASE: M051

KEY FINDINGS:
A pancreatic mass with biliary obstruction and very high lipase (1,592) shows pancreatic origin with obstructive cholestatic and hepatocellular injury.
Metastases are widespread: liver, bone, spleen, adrenal glands, peritoneum, retroperitoneum and mediastinal nodes. This is a highly aggressive tumour.
Immunohistochemistry is positive for cytokeratins (AE1/AE3, CAM5.2, CK7), synaptophysin, chromogranin A and TTF-1, which indicates epithelial neuroendocrine differentiation.
Ki-67 of 95% means a high-grade small cell carcinoma, not a well-differentiated tumour.
Normal CA19-9 argues against ductal adenocarcinoma.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Primary small cell (high-grade neuroendocrine) carcinoma of the pancreas
Metastatic small cell lung carcinoma to the pancreas
Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma
Well-differentiated pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour (G3)
Lymphoma
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Primary pancreatic small cell carcinoma
For: a dominant pancreatic mass with biliary obstruction and pancreatitis-range lipase, neuroendocrine markers, Ki-67 95%, and TTF-1 (which can be positive in extrapulmonary small cell carcinoma).
Against: TTF-1 and mediastinal/hilar nodes raise the possibility of a lung primary.
Metastatic small cell lung cancer
For: TTF-1 positivity and mediastinal/hilar lymphadenopathy.
Against: no lung primary is described, and the pancreatic mass is the dominant obstructing lesion.
Ductal adenocarcinoma
For: pancreatic mass, biliary obstruction and weight loss.
Against: neuroendocrine markers, TTF-1 and normal CA19-9.
Well-differentiated NET G3
For: synaptophysin and chromogranin.
Against: Ki-67 95% and TTF-1 favour poorly differentiated carcinoma.
Lymphoma
For: widespread nodal and visceral disease.
Against: cytokeratin and neuroendocrine positivity.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT: The immunoprofile excludes adenocarcinoma and lymphoma. The proliferation index excludes a well-differentiated NET. A lung primary cannot be fully excluded, but with no lung mass and a dominant obstructing pancreatic tumour I commit to primary pancreatic small cell carcinoma.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Small cell (poorly differentiated, high-grade neuroendocrine) carcinoma of the pancreas with widespread metastases

CASE: M052

KEY FINDINGS:
A one-year history of jaundice, intermittent upper abdominal pain and a palpable mass fits the classic presentation of a choledochal cyst.
Labs show conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia (direct 6.6) with raised transaminases, which means obstructive cholestasis.
MRCP shows fusiform cystic dilatation of both the intrahepatic and extrahepatic ducts, which is Todani type IVA.
Hepatosplenomegaly with splenic vein dilatation suggests portal hypertension from secondary biliary cirrhosis.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Choledochal cyst, Todani type IVA
Caroli disease/syndrome (type V)
Choledochal cyst type I with secondary intrahepatic dilatation
Primary sclerosing cholangitis
Hepatic hydatid cyst or biliary cystadenoma
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Type IVA choledochal cyst
For: multiple intrahepatic and extrahepatic cysts in continuity with the bile duct, the classic triad, and a female adolescent.
Against: none.
Caroli disease/syndrome
For: intrahepatic cystic dilatation, and portal hypertension (as in Caroli syndrome).
Against: Caroli disease is limited to intrahepatic ducts, and here there is fusiform extrahepatic dilatation.
Type I with secondary intrahepatic dilatation
For: an extrahepatic fusiform cyst.
Against: the intrahepatic lesions are cystic in their own right, not simple upstream dilatation.
Primary sclerosing cholangitis
For: cholestasis with intra- and extrahepatic involvement.
Against: cystic ectasia rather than beaded strictures, and her age.
Hydatid cyst or cystadenoma
For: cystic liver lesions.
Against: they communicate with the bile ducts in a ductal pattern, and there is no exposure history.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT: Extrahepatic involvement excludes Caroli disease. The imaging morphology excludes primary sclerosing cholangitis and parasitic or neoplastic cysts. I commit to Todani type IVA choledochal cyst.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Choledochal cyst, Todani type IVA, with obstructive jaundice and possible secondary portal hypertension

CASE: M053

KEY FINDINGS:
Short stature, a short neck, a low posterior hairline, a broad chest, primary amenorrhoea, a hypoplastic uterus and non-visualised ovaries form the Turner phenotype.
The karyotype is 46,X,i(X)(q10)/45,X, a mosaic isochromosome Xq that confirms Turner syndrome. Isochromosome Xq also carries an increased risk of hearing loss and autoimmune disease.
A suprasellar meningioma extends into the pituitary and causes low ACTH, cortisol and GH. This explains the hyponatraemia, hypoglycaemia and decreased responsiveness as secondary adrenal insufficiency.
Bilateral cataracts, a second meningioma in the cerebellopontine angle and hearing loss raise the question of NF2 as a coexisting condition.
LH and FSH are low (hypogonadotropic). This is atypical for Turner syndrome, where gonadotropins are normally high, and is explained by the pituitary compression.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Turner syndrome (mosaic i(Xq)) with suprasellar meningioma causing hypopituitarism
Neurofibromatosis type 2
Noonan syndrome
Isolated hypopituitarism from a sellar tumour (craniopharyngioma, pituitary adenoma)
Kallmann syndrome
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Turner with meningioma-induced hypopituitarism
For: the karyotype, the phenotype, streak gonads, hearing loss and the hormone profile.
Against: none decisive.
NF2
For: multiple meningiomas, cataracts and hearing loss.
Against: no vestibular schwannoma is reported, and NF2 does not explain the karyotype or the dysmorphism.
Noonan syndrome
For: short stature, a short neck and a broad chest.
Against: an abnormal X karyotype, and no cardiac lesion.
Isolated sellar tumour
For: panhypopituitarism.
Against: does not explain the dysmorphism or gonadal dysgenesis, and imaging shows meningioma.
Kallmann syndrome
For: hypogonadotropic hypogonadism.
Against: ACTH and GH are also deficient, and the karyotype is abnormal.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT: The karyotype excludes Noonan and Kallmann syndromes as primary diagnoses. Hypopituitarism is secondary to the meningioma. NF2 remains a consideration for genetic and audiological follow-up but is not proven. I commit to mosaic Turner syndrome with a suprasellar meningioma causing hypopituitarism and adrenal crisis.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Mosaic Turner syndrome (45,X/46,X,i(Xq)) complicated by a suprasellar meningioma causing hypopituitarism (secondary adrenal insufficiency), with a cerebellopontine angle meningioma

Tenderness over the posteromedial malleolus with pain on passive dorsiflexion points to a posterior tibial plafond injury.

Isolated posterior malleolus fracture
Trimalleolar fracture or equivalent
Maisonneuve injury
Posterior pilon fracture
Posterior talar process fracture or Achilles rupture

Isolated posterior malleolus fracture

Trimalleolar fracture

CASE: M055

KEY FINDINGS:
Painless jaundice with a cholestatic picture (ALP 546, bilirubin 8.7) indicates biliary obstruction.

Type I choledochal cyst with pancreaticobiliary maljunction complicated by cholangiocarcinoma
Choledocholithiasis with benign stricture
Primary IPNB-associated cholangiocarcinoma without a congenital cyst
Pancreatic head or ampullary carcinoma
Primary sclerosing cholangitis with cholangiocarcinoma

Choledochal cyst with maljunction and cholangiocarcinoma
For: all the imaging and pathology findings.

CASE: M056

KEY FINDINGS:
Prenatal upper small bowel atresia (dilated stomach and jejunum) is the known association for umbilical cord ulcer. Refluxed bile and gastric contents in the amniotic fluid erode the cord.
A teardrop-shaped haematoma next to the cord on MRI indicates cord vessel bleeding.
Decreased fetal movement led to fetal death, with 1,300 mL of bloody amniotic fluid and a pale fetus. This means fetal exsanguination from fetal vessels, not maternal haemorrhage.
The cord itself showed ulceration, loss of Wharton's jelly, and degenerated, haemorrhagic vessels.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Umbilical cord ulcer associated with congenital intestinal (jejunal) atresia, causing fetal haemorrhage and intrauterine death
Placental abruption
Ruptured vasa praevia
Massive fetomaternal haemorrhage
Spontaneous umbilical cord haematoma
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Cord ulcer with atresia
For: intestinal atresia, bloody amniotic fluid, a pale fetus, and ulcerated cord vessels.
Against: none.
Placental abruption
For: bloody fluid and fetal death.
Against: the haemorrhage is fetal, there are cord lesions, and no retroplacental clot is described.
Vasa praevia
For: fetal exsanguination.
Against: no membranous vessels are described, and bleeding was intra-amniotic, not vaginal at rupture of membranes.
Fetomaternal haemorrhage
For: a pale fetus.
Against: the blood is in the amniotic fluid, and the cord is ulcerated.
Spontaneous cord haematoma
For: a haematoma near the cord.
Against: ulceration, the atresia association, and free intra-amniotic bleeding.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT: The gross cord pathology and the atresia exclude the alternatives. I commit to umbilical cord ulcer secondary to upper small bowel atresia.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Umbilical cord ulcer associated with congenital upper small bowel (jejunal) atresia, causing fetal haemorrhage and intrauterine fetal death
