CASE: M113

KEY FINDINGS:
Six months of cough and dyspnea with only partial response to steroids and antibiotics. This is a chronic, non-infectious-appearing course.
Milky white BAL fluid, which is the classic sign of alveolar lipoproteinaceous material.
Crazy-paving pattern on CT: diffuse ground-glass opacities with septal thickening.
Restrictive defect with reduced DLCO, hypoxemia, and a raised A-a gradient. These reflect impaired gas exchange from filled alveoli.
Biopsy showing granular eosinophilic, PAS-positive material filling the alveoli. This is pathognomonic for pulmonary alveolar proteinosis (PAP).
Raised LDH, CEA, and Cyfra21-1. These are typical PAP serum markers, released from surfactant-laden alveoli.
GM-CSF autoantibody 4.5 μg/mL, above the 4 μg/mL threshold. This supports the autoimmune form.
Positive bacterial culture, indicating a superimposed infection. Macrophage dysfunction in PAP predisposes to opportunists such as Nocardia and mycobacteria.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Autoimmune PAP with secondary infection
Secondary PAP (infection-, malignancy-, or dust-related)
Invasive mucinous adenocarcinoma / lepidic lung cancer
Interstitial lung disease related to connective tissue disease (ANA-positive)
Pneumocystis or other infectious pneumonia
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Autoimmune PAP.
For: milky BAL, crazy-paving, PAS-positive alveolar filling, high LDH/CEA/Cyfra21-1, GM-CSF antibody above threshold, typical age.
Against: the antibody level is only borderline.
Secondary PAP.
For: a positive culture.
Against: no hematologic malignancy, dust exposure, or immunosuppressive disease. The infection is better explained as a complication of PAP.
Mucinous adenocarcinoma.
For: high CEA and Cyfra21-1; ground-glass opacities.
Against: biopsy showed acellular proteinaceous material without tumor; milky BAL.
CTD-ILD.
For: ANA 1:80; restrictive physiology.
Against: a low-titer ANA is nonspecific, there are no systemic CTD features, and the histology is not an ILD pattern.
Pneumocystis / infectious pneumonia.
For: ground-glass opacities.
Against: six-month afebrile course, negative pathogen screening, and the specific PAP histology.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
I exclude malignancy and CTD-ILD on the histology.
I exclude primary infection because of the chronicity and because the histology explains the picture; the culture represents superinfection.
I exclude secondary PAP because no underlying cause is present.
I commit to autoimmune PAP complicated by infection.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Pulmonary alveolar proteinosis (autoimmune, GM-CSF antibody positive) with superimposed bacterial infection (likely an opportunist such as Nocardia).

CASE: M114

KEY FINDINGS:
A small right hemithorax with cardiac dextroposition and ipsilateral tracheal shift. This means the right lung is congenitally small, not that the heart is primarily misplaced.
Hypoplastic right lung with a hypoplastic right pulmonary artery.
Anomalous right pulmonary venous drainage into the infradiaphragmatic IVC. This is the "scimitar" vein.
Systemic arterial supply from the abdominal aorta to the right lower lobe.
A hyparterial bilobed right bronchus, i.e., left-sided bronchial isomerism of the right lung.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Scimitar syndrome (congenital pulmonary venolobar / hypogenetic lung syndrome)
Isolated partial anomalous pulmonary venous return
Pulmonary sequestration
Unilateral pulmonary hypoplasia/agenesis of another cause
Acquired right lung volume loss (collapse, fibrosis, post-infectious)
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Scimitar syndrome.
For: the full constellation is present (anomalous venous return to the IVC, right lung hypoplasia, PA hypoplasia, systemic arterial supply, bronchial anomaly, dextroposition).
Against: none.
Isolated PAPVR.
For: the anomalous vein.
Against: does not explain the hypoplasia or the systemic arterial supply.
Sequestration.
For: aortic arterial supply.
Against: the lobe connects to the bronchial tree, and there is a whole-lung developmental anomaly with anomalous venous drainage.
Other hypoplasia.
For: small lung.
Against: the specific vascular anomalies are not explained.
Acquired volume loss.
For: restrictive picture.
Against: no tuberculosis, smoking, or exposure history; the vascular and bronchial anomalies are congenital.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT: Only scimitar syndrome unifies all the vascular, bronchial, and parenchymal anomalies. The other candidates each explain only part of the picture, so I commit to scimitar syndrome.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Scimitar syndrome (hypogenetic right lung / congenital pulmonary venolobar syndrome).

CASE: M115

KEY FINDINGS:
New LV systolic dysfunction (EF 41%, fractional shortening 19%) at the end of pregnancy and peripartum. EF below 45% in this window is a defining criterion.
Pulmonary edema, cardiomegaly, and markedly raised BNP, indicating heart failure.
Risk factors: pre-eclampsia (sFlt-1/PlGF 77) and twin gestation. Both are strong risk factors through an anti-angiogenic mechanism.
Only a mild troponin rise, with sinus rhythm and PE excluded. This argues against ischemia and PE.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Peripartum cardiomyopathy (PPCM)
Pre-eclampsia-related pulmonary edema / hypertensive heart failure without cardiomyopathy
Iatrogenic fluid overload
Myocarditis or pregnancy-associated MI/SCAD
Pre-existing undiagnosed cardiomyopathy
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
PPCM.
For: timing, EF 41%, heart failure, pre-eclampsia, twins.
Against: none decisive. Recovery and follow-up will confirm.
Pre-eclampsia pulmonary edema.
For: pre-eclampsia, diastolic dysfunction.
Against: the reduced systolic EF goes beyond what pre-eclampsia alone typically causes.
Fluid overload.
For: peri-operative state.
Against: does not reduce EF.
Myocarditis / MI / SCAD.
For: troponin rise.
Against: the rise is only mild, ECG shows sinus rhythm, and there are no symptoms.
Pre-existing cardiomyopathy.
For: possible in principle.
Against: no prior history in a young woman with three pregnancies.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
PE is excluded by CTPA.
Ischemia is unlikely.
Fluid overload and pre-eclampsia alone do not explain the systolic dysfunction.
I commit to PPCM.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Peripartum cardiomyopathy (associated with pre-eclampsia and twin pregnancy).

CASE: M116

KEY FINDINGS:
Documented hypoglycemia below 60 mg/dL with symptoms that resolve with treatment (Whipple's triad), in both fasting and postprandial states on CGM.
Normal insulin, proinsulin, and C-peptide with appropriate ketogenesis during hypoglycemia. This excludes hyperinsulinemic causes such as insulinoma, sulfonylurea use, and insulin autoimmunity.
Normal cortisol and imaging. This excludes adrenal insufficiency and tumors.
hEDS (Beighton 9/9) with POTS, dysautonomia, and small fiber neuropathy. Autonomic dysfunction disrupts the counter-regulatory response and gut motility/absorption.
Non-epileptic seizures on video EEG. The "seizures" are symptomatic or functional, not epilepsy.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Hypoglycemia associated with hEDS/dysautonomia (non-insulin-mediated reactive and fasting hypoglycemia)
Insulinoma / endogenous hyperinsulinism (including NIPHS)
Adrenal insufficiency or other counter-regulatory hormone deficiency
Factitious hypoglycemia
Glycogen storage / fatty-acid oxidation disorder
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
hEDS/dysautonomia-associated hypoglycemia.
For: lifelong course, triggers (exertion, heat, meals, fasting), extensive dysautonomia, negative endocrine work-up.
Against: this is a diagnosis of exclusion.
Insulinoma / NIPHS.
For: postprandial and fasting hypoglycemia.
Against: normal β-cell peptides during hypoglycemia and normal CT.
Adrenal insufficiency.
For: orthostatic hypotension, fatigue.
Against: normal cortisol.
Factitious hypoglycemia.
For: recurrent unexplained episodes.
Against: suppressed insulin and C-peptide pattern, and many years of objectively documented episodes.
Inborn metabolic error.
For: fasting hypoglycemia.
Against: appropriate ketogenesis, onset in adolescence, normal hepatic imaging.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Hyperinsulinism, adrenal insufficiency, and factitious causes are excluded biochemically.
Metabolic disorders are excluded by the normal ketogenesis and the clinical course.
I commit to hypoglycemia secondary to hEDS-related autonomic dysfunction.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Recurrent non-insulin-mediated (reactive and fasting) hypoglycemia associated with hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos syndrome and dysautonomia; episodes are non-epileptic.

CASE: M117

KEY FINDINGS:
Abrupt, bilateral, rapidly progressive vision loss over one year in a young adult, with nyctalopia, photopsia, and field constriction. This suggests acquired photoreceptor injury.
Electronegative ERG with loss of outer retinal layers and photoreceptor segments on OCT. This means widespread photoreceptor/inner retinal dysfunction.
Attenuated vessels and disc pallor with no bone spicules, and pinpoint leakage with perivascular staining on angiography. These point to an inflammatory (vasculitic) retinopathy rather than a classic dystrophy.
Negative serology, failure of antivirals, and a normal PET. Infection and paraneoplastic disease are ruled out.
Consanguinity raises the possibility of an inherited retinal dystrophy.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Non-paraneoplastic autoimmune retinopathy (npAIR)
Inherited retinal dystrophy (retinitis pigmentosa sine pigmento, cone-rod dystrophy)
Paraneoplastic retinopathy (CAR/MAR)
Infectious retinitis (syphilis, viral)
Birdshot chorioretinopathy / other posterior uveitis
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
npAIR.
For: abrupt, rapid bilateral loss, photopsia, electronegative ERG, vascular leakage, no tumor, no infection.
Against: autoantibodies were not demonstrated (anti-retinal antibody testing is often unavailable or negative), and response to steroids was poor.
Inherited dystrophy.
For: consanguinity, nyctalopia, attenuated vessels.
Against: abrupt onset at 30, no family history, no bone spicules, inflammatory leakage.
Paraneoplastic retinopathy.
For: similar phenotype.
Against: normal PET.
Infectious retinitis.
For: perivascular staining.
Against: negative serology, no antiviral response.
Birdshot / uveitis.
For: leakage, disc staining, electronegative ERG.
Against: no characteristic lesions and no vitritis described.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT: Infection and malignancy are excluded. An inherited dystrophy fits poorly with the acute onset and inflammatory signs. I commit to npAIR.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Non-paraneoplastic autoimmune retinopathy.

CASE: M118

KEY FINDINGS:
Painful skull-base extramedullary mass with facial numbness and dysgeusia, i.e., cranial nerve (trigeminal) involvement.
Multiple lytic, PET-avid marrow lesions, IgG-κ M-band, raised κ free light chain, and κ/λ ratio of 9.2. These indicate a plasma cell neoplasm with end-organ (bone) damage.
Clonal plasma cells in the marrow, and a skull mass clonally distinct from the CLL. The mass belongs to the plasma cell process, not to Richter transformation.
A coexisting CD5+/CD23+ small B-cell clone in the marrow with normal blood counts: concurrent CLL/SLL (or MBL).
High-risk genetics (TP53/ATM gains, NF1) and a strong family history of lymphoid and other malignancy.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Multiple myeloma (IgG-κ) with extramedullary skull-base plasmacytoma, coexisting with CLL/SLL
Richter transformation (DLBCL) of CLL
Solitary plasmacytoma of bone
Metastatic carcinoma to the skull base
Lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Myeloma with plasmacytoma + CLL.
For: lytic lesions, M-protein, light chains, marrow plasma cell clone, mass distinct from the CLL clone.
Against: none major.
Richter transformation.
For: CLL is present, a hypermetabolic mass, family DLBCL history.
Against: the mass is clonally distinct from the CLL, and there are no CLL deletions in the blood.
Solitary plasmacytoma.
For: an isolated skull-base mass.
Against: multiple lytic lesions and marrow involvement.
Metastatic carcinoma.
For: lytic lesions, kidney cancer in the family.
Against: biopsy findings and the M-protein.
Lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma.
For: a B-cell process with an M-band.
Against: the paraprotein is IgG rather than IgM, and lytic lesions are present.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT:
Richter transformation is excluded by clonality.
Solitary plasmacytoma is excluded by the multifocal disease.
Carcinoma and lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma are excluded by histology and the immunoglobulin isotype.
I commit to myeloma with an extramedullary plasmacytoma, concurrent with CLL.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: IgG-κ multiple myeloma with extramedullary skull-base plasmacytoma, coexisting with CLL/SLL (two clonally distinct neoplasms).

CASE: M119

KEY FINDINGS:

Passive transfer of maternal antibodies without infection
Other congenital infections (rubella, CMV "celery-stalk" metaphyses)
Osteomyelitis (bacterial)
Metabolic bone disease of prematurity / rickets

Congenital syphilis.

CASE: M120

KEY FINDINGS:
Acute severe abdominal pain, vomiting, and a palpable mass in a multiparous-age woman. Laxity of the splenic ligaments, which is linked to multiparity, predisposes to wandering spleen.
CT shows no spleen in the left upper quadrant, an ectopic mass, and a whirlpool sign of twisted splenic vessels. These are diagnostic of torsion.
The mass is hypodense and nonenhancing, indicating infarction.
At surgery, the ectopic spleen was found in the mid-abdomen/right iliac fossa with a 720° pedicle torsion, and splenectomy was performed.
DIFFERENTIAL:
Torsion of a wandering spleen with splenic infarction
Appendiceal abscess
Ovarian torsion / adnexal mass
Mesenteric cyst or tumor
Splenosis / accessory spleen torsion
EVIDENCE FOR AND AGAINST:
Wandering spleen torsion.
For: absent orthotopic spleen, whirlpool sign, nonenhancing ectopic mass, surgical confirmation.
Against: none.
Appendiceal abscess.
For: right iliac fossa complex mass on ultrasound.
Against: CT shows splenic vessels and an absent spleen.
Ovarian torsion.
For: acute pain, a mass, a whirlpool sign.
Against: the twisted vessels are splenic.
Mesenteric mass.
For: a mid-abdominal mass.
Against: the vascular torsion and the absent spleen.
Accessory spleen torsion.
For: ectopic splenic tissue.
Against: there is no normal spleen; the whole organ is displaced.
EXCLUSIONS AND COMMITMENT: Imaging and surgery exclude abscess, adnexal, and mesenteric causes. The absence of an orthotopic spleen excludes accessory spleen. I commit to torsion of a wandering spleen.
FINAL DIAGNOSIS: Wandering (ectopic) spleen with 720° pedicle torsion and splenic infarction.
